# Maximalism

Four philosophies in this directory form one map. Minimalism and maximalism vary
**how much** is present; flat and brutalism vary **how finished** it looks.

```text
                      polished                     raw
   less               minimalism                  brutalist minimalism
   more               maximalism                  brutalist maximalism
```

Maximalism is **deliberate density and expression in service of the content.**
Where minimalism says _remove until the structure is clear_, maximalism says
**add until the structure is felt** — pattern, saturation, layering, ceremony,
scale. Density is the gift it offers the reader who wants everything.

**This is not "more stuff."** An over-decorated page with no hierarchy is noise,
not maximalism. The difference is whether the additions were _governed by a
rule_. Maximalism is ornament deployed as grammar; chaos is ornament deployed as
panic.

**Roots:** Baroque and Rococo interiors (ornament as total environment), Arts and
Crafts and Gujarati/Kutch embroidery (repetition as craft), psychedelic poster
work (Wes Wilson, Rick Griffin), the Memphis Group (Sottsass, 1981 —
maximalism as deliberate anti-corporate play), illuminated manuscripts and their
crowded marginalia, Mexican muralism, Art Nouveau, and Afrofuturist visual
culture. Contrast `design/philosophy/brutalism.md` for the same impulse without
the finish.

---

## 1. Core Principles

- **Ornament is structure** — decoration that repeats becomes a visual grammar.
  Filler decoration communicates nothing; patterned space communicates energy,
  place, and era.
- **Systematic or nothing** — repetition plus alignment. The same motif applied
  consistently reads as language; applied randomly it reads as an error.
- **Density as generosity** — a catalogue that shows everything rewards the reader
  who wants it. Hiding is the minimalism failure mode, and sometimes it is right.
- **Scale contrast is the engine** — drama comes from extreme size differences
  between elements. A maximalist layout with uniform sizes is merely busy.
- **Ceremony over recede** — symmetrical, frontal, layered composition. The page
  performs; it does not step aside.
- **Every surface earns pattern** — whitespace can itself be patterned. "Empty"
  is a choice, not a default.
- **The reader must still be able to find things** — see §2. This is the hard
  constraint, not an afterthought.

---

## 2. The One Non-Negotiable: Hierarchy

Maximalism fails in exactly one way that matters: **density without a reading
order becomes noise.** Before adding anything, fix the order the eye moves in.

- **One focal point, unmistakably.** The most saturated, largest, or most
  detailed element on the screen. Exactly one.
- **Three levels, as always** — focal, supporting, ambient. Density is permitted
  between levels; ambiguity is not.
- **If two elements compete, the composition is broken** — not the audience's
  problem to solve.

Ask before applying maximalism: _is this added because the content needs it, or
because a decision about hierarchy was avoided?_ The second is decoration as
avoidance, which is the most common misuse.

---

## 3. Zone Strategy

The technique that makes maximalism buildable. Pattern and text cannot share a
surface at high frequency — the pattern destroys reading.

Divide the canvas into zones and commit to each one:

| Zone                | Content                                     |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Pattern field**   | Dense motif, full saturation, no text       |
| **Quiet pocket**    | Calm or solid — text lives here             |
| **Image field**     | Photography, duotone-treated to the palette |
| **Ornamental band** | Rules, corner marks, separators             |

- **Every screen needs at least one quiet pocket.** Not for looks — the eye needs
  somewhere to rest, and body text needs contrast to survive.
- **Separate zones with a hard edge** — a rule, a solid band, or a step in
  pattern frequency. A gradual fade between pattern and text will not read.
- **Text on a pattern needs a panel.** Solid fill, low-opacity scrim, or a
  low-frequency pattern behind it. Never body text directly on a high-frequency
  motif.
- **Pattern in the margins, discipline in the centre.** The most reliable
  composition: dense ornament framing a quiet text core.

---

## 4. Colour

- **Many hues are permitted when governed by a rule.** Pick one system and hold
  it: a base hue plus complementary accents, or a fixed triad. Saturation without
  a scheme is noise.
- **Saturation contrast carries hierarchy.** The focal element is the most
  saturated thing on screen. Equal saturation everywhere means no focal point.
- **Limit fully-saturated colours to one focal zone** — roughly three at once,
  no more.
- **Duotone everything photographic.** Tinting images into the palette is what
  makes a dense composition read as one piece rather than a collage.
- **Neutrals still earn their place** — a small neutral field gives dense colour
  somewhere to rest.

---

## 5. Pattern

- **Repetition unit must be small enough to read as texture** and large enough to
  be seen as motif. Below ~8px it is noise; above ~48px it is illustration.
- **One motif at four scales is richer than four motifs at one scale.** Scaling
  the same unit is the cheapest path to complexity.
- **Tile seamlessly or bound it deliberately.** A half-pattern that stops
  arbitrarily is the tell of an unfinished page.
- **Motif sources that work:** geometric, botanical, mechanical, typographic.
  Sketched organic motifs need far more craft than they appear to.
- **Reserve the highest frequency for the smallest areas.** Frequency competes
  with scale for attention; large areas need coarse pattern.

---

## 6. Composition

- **Symmetrical and frontal by default** — the opposite of
  `design/philosophy/minimalism.md`, which favours deliberate asymmetry. Ceremony
  needs an axis.
- **Break the symmetry once, deliberately.** A single off-axis element is a focal
  point. Two is noise.
- **Borders are architecture.** Thick rules, double rules, corner marks and
  frames do structural work that flat compositions delegate to whitespace.
- **Layer rather than space.** Overlapping panels, offset prints, stacked frames —
  depth comes from composition, not only shadow.
- **Let the edge be designed.** The page boundary, corner treatments, and the
  transition into the fold are all composition.

---

## 7. Typography

- **Display face does the drama.** Maximalism usually needs a characterful
  headline face; a neutral grotesque will not carry a dense page.
- **Extreme scale range** — a display size several times the body size. Relative
  steps read as timid on a maximalist page.
- **Pair at most two families**, ideally a display face plus one text face with
  real character. Ornament in type replaces ornament in layout.
- **Body text stays readable and unbothered** — it gets the quiet pocket, its own
  measure, and no pattern. Legibility is where maximalism fails first.

---

## 8. Motion

- **Choreograph, do not ambient-animate.** Maximalism allows more motion than
  minimalism, but as _sequences_ — staggered arrivals, ordered reveals — not as
  constant drift.
- **Longer durations are appropriate** — 400–800ms for theatrical reveals. Fast
  motion reads as responsive; slow reads as considered. Pick one deliberately.
- **Staggering is the signature** — elements arriving in sequence is the maximalist
  equivalent of density.
- **Never animate for atmosphere on a task surface.** Ornamental motion on a
  form is a defect regardless of taste.

---

## 9. Where Maximalism Works

- **Brand and identity surfaces** — the page is the brand statement. This is the
  home turf.
- **Editorial and cultural** — music, film, food, fashion, art, publishing.
  Richness of the content is the justification.
- **Rich product categories** — coffee, whisky, wine, records, sneakers. Detail,
  provenance, and texture are what the buyer came for.
- **Campaigns, launches, anniversaries** — time-boxed, so the density costs
  nothing.
- **Portfolio and showcase sites** — the work is the ornament's subject.
- **Print, packaging, poster, album art** — the medium was built for it.
- **Deliberate subversion** — when the category is uniformly beige and you are
  not. Contrast is part of the strategy.

---

## 10. Where It Fails

- **Operational and data-dense tools** — density actively harms. Use
  `design/philosophy/flat.md` or `design/philosophy/minimalism.md`.
- **Tasks with a completion criteria** — checkout, forms, settings. Ornament
  competes with the task and users pay for it in errors.
- **Thin content** — maximalism over poor content reads as desperation, because
  the ornament is carrying weight nothing else will.
- **Audiences who came for speed** — utilitarian users read decoration as cost.
- **Trust-sensitive and enterprise surfaces** — density reads as lack of control.
- **Performance-constrained targets** — patterns, large imagery, and motion are
  expensive, and the audience is often on poor hardware or poor connections.
- **Accessibility budgets** — this is the real constraint, see below.

---

## 11. The Accessibility Cost

Maximalism spends the accessibility budget faster than any other philosophy here.
These are not negotiable:

- **Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1, large text and UI boundaries ≥ 3:1.** Pattern and
  saturated backgrounds destroy contrast; verify the actual composited result,
  not the text colour in isolation.
- **Pattern is never the sole carrier of meaning.** Textures that encode state
  need a redundant cue — text, shape, or position.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` honoured** — and on maximalist motion this matters
  most, since staggered theatrical sequences are exactly what triggers vestibular
  symptoms.
- **Text remains resizable to 200%** without the pattern overlapping it. Fixed
  decorative layers are where this breaks first.
- **Never encode status in colour alone** — harder to honour when the whole page
  is saturated.

The honest framing: maximalism is the hardest of these four to make accessible,
because the aesthetic is in tension with the constraints rather than aligned with
them. Budget for it accordingly.

---

## 12. The Reconciliation: Chrome Maximal, Task Minimal

The sophisticated version, and the one most worth building. **Decorative surfaces
carry the expression; interactive surfaces are ruthlessly simple.**

- The brand shell — headers, hero, section transitions, empty states, marketing
  pages — runs at full maximalism.
- The functional interior — forms, checkout, settings, data entry, anything with
  a completion criteria — reverts to `design/philosophy/minimalism.md`.
- The transition between them should be intentional and legible, so the user knows
  the rules changed.

This lets a product be memorable without making its forms hard to use, and it
concentrates the craft budget where it will actually be seen.

## General Rules of Thumb

- **Systematic ornament is language; random ornament is noise.**
- **One focal point.** Density without hierarchy is failure, not style.
- **Every screen needs a quiet pocket**, and it is where the text goes.
- **Never body text on a high-frequency pattern** — put a panel behind it.
- **Saturation contrast is hierarchy** — the focal element is the most saturated.
- **One motif at four scales** beats four motifs at one.
- **Symmetrical and frontal by default**; break it once, deliberately.
- **Duotone the photography** so the composition reads as one piece.
- **Body text gets no ornament at all.** It is where maximalism fails first.
- **Motion is choreographed, not ambient** — stagger, don't drift.
- **Chrome maximal, task minimal** — the reconciliation worth building.
- **Accessibility is the bill.** Budget for it before designing, not after.

## Quick-Start Checklist

- [ ] Content is rich enough to justify the density (thin content fails this)
- [ ] Exactly one focal point identified; three hierarchy levels fixed
- [ ] Canvas divided into pattern / quiet / image / ornamental zones
- [ ] At least one quiet pocket per screen, reserved for body text
- [ ] Pattern and text separated by a hard edge, never a gradient
- [ ] Body text on a solid or scrimmed panel; composited contrast verified
- [ ] Colour scheme chosen and held; ≤ 3 fully-saturated colours in the focal zone
- [ ] Photography duotoned into the palette
- [ ] One motif at multiple scales, tiling seamlessly or deliberately bounded
- [ ] Composition symmetrical and frontal; asymmetry used once at most
- [ ] Display face does the drama; at most two families
- [ ] Motion choreographed and staggered; `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured
- [ ] Status never encoded by pattern or colour alone; text resizable to 200%
- [ ] Functional surfaces excluded from the maximalist treatment
