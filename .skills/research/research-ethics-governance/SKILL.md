# Research Ethics and Governance

## Purpose

Identify ethical responsibilities and governance decisions across research planning, conduct, analysis, and dissemination. This guide supports preparation; it is not legal advice, an ethics approval, or a substitute for institutional and jurisdiction-specific requirements.

## Workflow

1. Describe purpose, methods, participants/materials, data, risks, and intended outputs.
2. Identify applicable oversight bodies, approvals, registrations, agreements, and reporting duties.
3. Assess participant welfare, consent, privacy, fairness, accessibility, and power relationships.
4. Define responsibility for conflicts of interest, authorship, supervision, and data access.
5. Plan safeguards, monitoring, incident response, amendments, and withdrawal or complaints.
6. Verify approvals and permissions before recruitment, intervention, or restricted data access.
7. Maintain records and reassess when the protocol, team, data, or intended use changes.

## Ethical review and consent

Do not self-declare that review is unnecessary. Seek a formal determination from the authorized body when required or uncertain. Consent should be informed, voluntary, understandable, and appropriate to the population and activity; document approved alternatives, waivers, or assent accurately.

Consider participant burden, foreseeable harm, distress, incidental findings, withdrawal limits, and support or referral pathways. For secondary or public data, check permitted use, re-identification risk, consent scope, and governance.

## Research integrity and relationships

Disclose relevant financial and nonfinancial interests and manage them transparently. Agree on authorship expectations and contribution records early, revisiting them as work evolves. Do not fabricate, falsify, plagiarize, selectively suppress results, or misrepresent approvals, contributions, or deviations.

## Completion checks

- Required review and permissions are confirmed by the responsible authority.
- Consent, welfare, privacy, and risk safeguards fit the participants and methods.
- Conflicts, roles, authorship, and data responsibilities are documented.
- Amendment, incident, withdrawal, and complaint pathways are known.
- Public reporting accurately describes oversight and limitations.
