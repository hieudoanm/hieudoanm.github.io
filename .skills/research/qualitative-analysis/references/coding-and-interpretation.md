# Coding and Interpretation

## Prepare the material

Document transcription, translation, cleaning, segmentation, and any software-assisted transformations. Preserve an original copy and protect identifiers. Record whether pauses, interaction, images, or other context are analytically relevant.

## Develop codes

Define the coding unit and approach. Codes may be semantic or latent, descriptive or interpretive, inductive or theory-informed. Keep definitions useful but revisable. Compare across cases while retaining each account's context.

## Build interpretations

Move from coded segments to patterns, categories, themes, processes, or explanations appropriate to the approach. Ask what the pattern means, under what conditions it appears, whose perspective is represented, and what complicates it.

Search for variation and disconfirming material. A theme is not automatically important because it is frequent; a rare case can be analytically consequential. If counts are reported, define the denominator and do not generalize beyond the sampling design.

## Use excerpts responsibly

Check quotation accuracy, surrounding context, translation, and disclosure risk. Paraphrase or mask details only with a documented rationale that preserves meaning and does not misrepresent the speaker.
