# Trustworthiness and Reporting

Rigor is demonstrated through a coherent relationship among question, approach, data, analysis, and claims—not by accumulating techniques.

## Strengthen the analytic account

- Preserve an audit trail of consequential decisions.
- Use sufficient, varied evidence to support each interpretation.
- Examine negative cases and competing explanations.
- Describe context and sampling so readers can judge transferability.
- Explain researcher roles and relationships to participants or materials.
- Report limitations in data generation, access, translation, and analysis.

Use peer dialogue, triangulation, participant engagement, or multiple analysts only where they fit the question and epistemology. None is a universal validity test. Participant feedback can add perspective but does not automatically determine the correct interpretation.

## Reporting

State the qualitative approach and rationale, context, recruitment and sample, data generation, researcher characteristics or reflexivity, analytic steps, ethics, and how findings are evidenced. Follow current field-specific reporting guidance when applicable.

Distinguish participant accounts, researcher interpretation, and contextual inference. Avoid unsupported claims of saturation, representativeness, consensus, or causality.
