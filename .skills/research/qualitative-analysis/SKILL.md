# Qualitative Analysis

## Purpose

Develop a credible, context-sensitive interpretation of qualitative material through a transparent analytic process. Analysis is not simply labeling text or counting themes; it involves reasoned interpretation grounded in the research question, data, and analytic tradition.

## Choose and state an approach

Select an approach that fits the question and epistemology. Examples include reflexive thematic analysis, framework analysis, qualitative content analysis, grounded theory, and discourse analysis. They have different aims and assumptions; do not combine incompatible procedures without explanation. See [approach selection](references/approach-selection.md).

## Workflow

1. Revisit the question, context, sampling, data-generation process, and intended contribution.
2. Confirm consent, permitted use, de-identification, secure access, and retention.
3. Familiarize with the material; record initial observations and positionality.
4. Develop codes or analytic concepts suited to the approach; preserve context and negative cases.
5. Compare within and across cases; refine patterns, categories, themes, or explanations.
6. Keep analytic memos, codebook or framework versions, and decision records.
7. Test interpretations against the material and plausible alternatives.
8. Select quotations or excerpts with context, confidentiality, and fair representation in mind.
9. Report the analytic process, researcher role, evidence, limitations, and transferability boundaries.

## Coding is not the conclusion

Code labels organize material; themes or explanations require an interpretive claim about patterned meaning relevant to the question. Frequencies may help describe a corpus but do not by themselves establish importance, prevalence, or representativeness.

## Reflexivity and rigor

Make the researcher's role and assumptions visible. Use techniques appropriate to the chosen approach: reflexive memoing, a transparent audit trail, framework comparison, peer dialogue, or participant engagement where justified. Do not treat inter-coder agreement or member checking as universal requirements; explain why a technique fits the approach.

## Reporting

Support interpretations with carefully selected, accurately transcribed evidence. Include variation and disconfirming cases where relevant. Protect identities, avoid decontextualized quotes, and distinguish participant accounts from researcher interpretation.

## Completion checks

- Question, context, data source, analytic approach, and researcher role are clear.
- Codes and interpretations have traceable links to material.
- Context, variation, and alternative interpretations were considered.
- Ethical handling and confidentiality are maintained.
- Claims are appropriately bounded and supported by evidence.
