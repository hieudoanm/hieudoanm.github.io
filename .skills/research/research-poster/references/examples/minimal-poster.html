Does Sleep Duration Predict Cognitive Performance?

H. Doan · Cognitive Neuroscience · University of Birmingham

Background

Sleep is associated with multiple aspects of cognition, including attention, memory, and executive function.

Research question

Is habitual sleep duration associated with cognitive performance?

Dataset

Behavioural and self-reported sleep measures were collected from 420 adult participants.

Analysis

  1. Inspect distributions.
  2. Estimate the sleep–performance association.
  3. Assess uncertainty using permutation analysis.

Main finding

+

Longer reported sleep duration was associated with better cognitive performance.

Illustrative figure for demonstrating a minimal poster layout. The displayed relationship is not intended to represent a real dataset.

Interpretation

The observed association is consistent with a relationship between sleep duration and cognitive performance.

Because the data are observational, the result does not establish that changing sleep duration would cause a change in cognition.

Limitations

  • Sleep duration was self-reported.
  • The study was observational.
  • Unmeasured variables may contribute to the association.
  • The sample may not represent the wider population.

Takeaway

The data support an association between sleep duration and cognitive performance, but not a causal conclusion.

Next steps

  • Use objective sleep measurements.
  • Investigate longitudinal changes.
  • Control for additional behavioural factors.
  • Test specific cognitive domains separately.
