Background

Example background copy only. Replace with claims supported by cited sources. Sleep is associated with multiple aspects of cognition, including attention, memory, and executive function. Chronic sleep deprivation has been linked to impaired cognitive performance, reduced emotional regulation, and increased risk of neurodegenerative diseases. However, the relationship between habitual sleep duration and cognitive performance in healthy adults remains debated.

Theoretical frameworks suggest that sleep supports memory consolidation through neural replay and synaptic pruning. Adequate sleep may also facilitate optimal functioning of attentional networks and executive control systems. These mechanisms predict a positive association between sleep duration and cognitive performance.

Research question

Is habitual sleep duration associated with cognitive performance?

Limitations

  • Sleep duration was self-reported, subject to recall bias.
  • The study was observational; causal inference is limited.
  • Unmeasured variables may contribute to the association.
  • The sample may not represent the wider population.
  • Single time-point measurement does not capture sleep variability.
  • Cognitive assessment was brief and may not capture all domains.
  • No objective sleep measures (actigraphy, polysomnography) were used.
