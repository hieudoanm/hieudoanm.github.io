# Research Writing

A skill for writing, revising, and evaluating scientific research documents.

The skill focuses on clear scientific reasoning, evidence-based claims, appropriate research article structure, and precise communication of methods, results, and interpretations.

## Use This Skill When

Use this skill when:

- Writing a research article
- Writing an MSc dissertation
- Writing a research report
- Writing a research proposal
- Writing an Introduction
- Writing a Methods section
- Writing a Results section
- Writing a Discussion
- Writing a Conclusion
- Writing or revising an abstract
- Improving scientific prose
- Turning research notes into a structured manuscript
- Checking whether claims are supported by evidence
- Checking whether a conclusion follows from the results
- Reviewing the structure of a scientific manuscript

## Core Framework

Scientific writing should maintain a clear chain:

```text
Research question
       ↓
Evidence
       ↓
Analysis
       ↓
Results
       ↓
Interpretation
       ↓
Conclusion
```

The central IMRaD framework is:

```text
Introduction → Why?
Methods      → How?
Results      → What?
Discussion   → So what?
```

## Article Structure

A typical empirical research article contains:

```text
Title
Abstract
Introduction
Methods
Results
Discussion
Conclusion
References
```

The exact structure may vary between journals, disciplines, and article types.

## Core Principles

### 1. Match Claims to Evidence

Never make a scientific claim stronger than the evidence supports.

```text
Observed
   ↓
Associated with
   ↓
Consistent with
   ↓
Suggests
   ↓
Supports
   ↓
Demonstrates
   ↓
Causes
```

Moving upward requires stronger evidence.

### 2. Separate Observation From Interpretation

Results describe what was observed.

Discussion explains what those observations might mean.

```text
Observation:
The model decoded condition X above chance.

Interpretation:
This suggests that the neural signal contains information
related to condition X.
```

Do not present an interpretation as though it were a direct observation.

### 3. Build Arguments From Evidence

Use:

```text
Claim
→ Evidence
→ Reasoning
→ Conclusion
```

Major claims should be supported by appropriate evidence or by the results of the current study.

### 4. Write Paragraphs Around One Idea

A strong scientific paragraph generally follows:

```text
Topic sentence
→ Evidence
→ Explanation
→ Connection to argument
```

Each paragraph should have a clear purpose.

### 5. Cite Sources Appropriately

Use primary research papers when discussing specific findings, methods, datasets, or original theories.

Use review papers for broad background, field-level consensus, and theoretical overviews.

Always verify that the cited source actually supports the claim.

## Recommended Workflow

```text
Understand the research
        ↓
Identify the research question
        ↓
Identify the research gap
        ↓
Identify the evidence
        ↓
Build the argument
        ↓
Create the article structure
        ↓
Draft
        ↓
Add and verify citations
        ↓
Check claim strength
        ↓
Check scientific accuracy
        ↓
Edit for clarity
        ↓
Final consistency check
```

Scientific correctness should be established before optimising prose.

## Quality Checklist

Before finalising a research document, check:

### Research

- Is the research question clear?
- Is the research gap clear?
- Are the hypotheses or objectives explicit?
- Do the methods address the research question?
- Do the results answer the research question?
- Does the conclusion follow from the evidence?

### Evidence

- Are major claims supported?
- Do citations actually support the claims?
- Are appropriate primary sources used?
- Are causal claims justified?
- Is uncertainty represented appropriately?
- Are statistical and practical significance distinguished?

### Structure

- Does the Introduction lead logically to the research question?
- Are the Methods sufficiently detailed?
- Are Results separated from interpretation?
- Does the Discussion interpret the findings?
- Are limitations addressed?
- Are figures and tables referenced appropriately?

### Writing

- Does each paragraph have one main idea?
- Is terminology consistent?
- Are sentences clear and precise?
- Is unnecessary jargon removed?
- Is unnecessary repetition removed?
- Does claim strength match evidence?

## Scope

This skill focuses on **scientific writing and reasoning**, not on replacing domain-specific methodological guidance.

When writing about a specialised field such as neuroimaging, computational neuroscience, statistics, or machine learning, use appropriate domain references and verify technical claims against authoritative sources.
