# Scientific Writing

## Purpose
This reference provides general principles for clear, precise, and effective scientific writing.

It should be used when drafting or reviewing research manuscripts, reports, dissertations, and other scientific documents.

---

## Make the Research Question Central
A research article should have a clear intellectual focus.

The research question should connect the major sections:

```text
Research question
       ↓
Study design
       ↓
Analysis
       ↓
Results
       ↓
Interpretation
       ↓
Conclusion
```

When reviewing a manuscript, ask:

> Can I identify the research question?

Then ask:

> Can I see how every major section contributes to answering it?

Material that does not contribute to the research question should be reconsidered.

---

## Distinguish Facts, Results, Interpretations, and Speculation
Scientific writing contains different levels of epistemic certainty.

### Established evidence

> Previous studies have reported...

### Current observation

> We observed...

### Interpretation

> This finding suggests...

### Hypothesis or speculation

> One possibility is that...

These categories should not be blurred.

A common writing error is to convert:

> The data are consistent with X.

into:

> The data demonstrate X.

The second statement makes a stronger claim.

---

## Write With Uncertainty
Scientific knowledge is often uncertain.

Useful terms include:

- may
- might
- could
- suggests
- is consistent with
- provides evidence for
- is compatible with
- raises the possibility

However, excessive hedging can make writing vague.

Compare:

> The results may possibly suggest that there could potentially be an association.

with:

> The results suggest an association.

Use the strongest language that is justified by the evidence.

---

## Make Methods Reproducible
A reader should be able to determine:

- What data were collected or used
- Who or what was studied
- What procedures were performed
- How the data were processed
- What analyses were conducted
- What statistical criteria were used

For computational research, reproducibility may additionally require:

- Software
- Package versions
- Model specifications
- Parameter settings
- Random seeds where relevant
- Data preprocessing decisions
- Code or repository information where available

Do not omit decisions simply because they seem obvious to the researcher.

---

## Report Results Without Interpretation
The Results section should establish what the analysis found.

A useful reporting sequence is:

```text
Question / analysis
      ↓
Method or comparison
      ↓
Estimate
      ↓
Uncertainty / statistical result
      ↓
Relevant figure or table
```

Interpretation should generally be reserved for the Discussion.

However, Results writing can still be selective: report the findings relevant to the research questions rather than every output produced by an analysis pipeline.

---

## Final Principle
The most important rule is:

> **Write exactly what the evidence supports—no more and no less.**

Good scientific writing makes the reasoning visible:

```text
Question
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Analysis
   ↓
Finding
   ↓
Interpretation
   ↓
Conclusion
```

The reader should be able to follow this chain without having to reconstruct the argument themselves.
