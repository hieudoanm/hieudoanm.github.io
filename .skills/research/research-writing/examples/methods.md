# Example: Writing a Methods Section

## Research Topic

**Topic:** Speech complexity and age in children

**Research question:** How does speech complexity change with age in typically developing children?

---

## Methods

### Dataset and Participants

The study used publicly available children's language data from the Edmonton Narrative Norms Instrument (ENNI). The dataset contains language samples from children across a range of ages and includes measures relevant to narrative language development.

The analysis focused on children classified as typically developing. Children with language impairment were excluded from the primary analysis because the research question concerned the relationship between age and speech complexity during typical language development.

### Measures

Age was treated as the primary predictor variable. Speech complexity was quantified using a predefined measure derived from the children's language samples.

The outcome measure was calculated consistently across participants using the same processing procedure. Higher values represented greater speech complexity.

### Data Processing

The data were inspected before analysis to identify missing observations, invalid values, and potential outliers. Observations that did not meet the predefined inclusion criteria were excluded from the analysis.

The distribution of the speech-complexity measure was examined using descriptive statistics and visualisations before conducting the main analysis.

### Statistical Analysis

The relationship between age and speech complexity was examined using a statistical model appropriate for a continuous outcome variable.

The analysis estimated whether speech complexity changed systematically with age. The direction and magnitude of the association were reported together with an appropriate measure of uncertainty.

A permutation analysis was also conducted to assess whether the observed relationship was stronger than would be expected under a null distribution generated by randomly rearranging the relationship between age and speech complexity.

All analyses were conducted using reproducible code.

## Structure of the Methods

The Methods section follows the logic:

```text
Who or what was studied?
        ↓
What variables were measured?
        ↓
How was the data processed?
        ↓
What analysis was performed?
```

The reader should be able to understand exactly how the study moved from raw observations to the reported results.

---

## What This Example Demonstrates

### 1. Describe what was actually done

The Methods section should report the implemented procedure, not what the researcher intended to do originally if the two differ.

### 2. Define variables before analysing them

Readers need to know what the predictor and outcome represent before interpreting the statistical analysis.

### 3. Separate processing from analysis

Data cleaning and preprocessing can affect the final results and should therefore be reported separately from the statistical model where appropriate.

### 4. Avoid results in the Methods

Do not write:

> Age significantly predicted speech complexity.

That is a result.

Instead write:

> The relationship between age and speech complexity was examined using a statistical model appropriate for a continuous outcome variable.

### 5. Provide enough detail for reproducibility

A reader should have enough information to understand how the analysis was performed and, where the required data and code are available, reproduce it.

---

## Methods Template

A general structure is:

```text
### Participants / Dataset

Describe the population, sample, or data source.

### Materials / Measures

Define the variables, instruments, stimuli, or measurements.

### Procedure

Describe what participants or researchers did.

### Data Processing

Describe cleaning, preprocessing, exclusions, transformations, and derived variables.

### Statistical Analysis

Describe the statistical models, tests, assumptions, correction procedures,
and uncertainty estimation.
```

Not every study requires every subsection.

The structure should reflect the actual research design.

---

## Common Problems

### Too vague

> The data were cleaned and analysed.

This does not tell the reader what was done.

### Better

> Observations with missing age or speech-complexity measurements were excluded before analysis. The distribution of the outcome variable was then examined using descriptive statistics and visualisations.

---

### Reporting results in Methods

> The regression showed a significant positive relationship between age and speech complexity.

This belongs in the Results section.

### Better

> A regression model was used to estimate the relationship between age and speech complexity.

---

### Describing software without describing the analysis

> The analysis was conducted in Python.

Software alone does not explain the methodology.

### Better

> The analysis was conducted in Python using a regression model to estimate the association between age and speech complexity. The analysis code was retained to support reproducibility.

---

## Methods Quality Checklist

- [ ] Is the dataset or participant sample clearly identified?
- [ ] Are inclusion and exclusion criteria reported?
- [ ] Are important variables defined?
- [ ] Are measurements described?
- [ ] Is the procedure clear?
- [ ] Are preprocessing steps reported?
- [ ] Are exclusions explained?
- [ ] Are statistical methods specified?
- [ ] Are assumptions or relevant analysis decisions reported?
- [ ] Are confirmatory and exploratory analyses distinguished?
- [ ] Could another researcher understand how the analysis was performed?
- [ ] Are results kept out of the Methods section?

---

## Key Principle

The Methods section should answer one question:

**"What exactly did the researchers do to obtain the evidence?"**
