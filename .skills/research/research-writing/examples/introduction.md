# Example: Writing a Research Introduction

## Research Topic

**Topic:** Speech complexity and age in children

**Research question:** How does speech complexity change with age in typically developing children?

---

## Example Introduction

### Introduction

Language develops rapidly during childhood, with children gradually becoming able to produce increasingly complex utterances. This development involves changes in vocabulary, grammar, sentence structure, and the organisation of speech. Understanding how these features change with age can provide insight into the typical trajectory of language development and provide a reference point for identifying atypical development.

Previous research has shown that children's language abilities generally become more sophisticated as they grow older. However, age-related changes may not occur uniformly across all aspects of language. Measures of speech complexity can therefore provide a more specific way to characterise developmental changes than broad measures of language ability alone.

One approach to studying speech development is to analyse children's spontaneous language during structured elicitation tasks. Such data allow researchers to examine characteristics of children's spoken language while preserving meaningful variation between individuals. Quantitative measures of linguistic complexity can then be used to investigate whether speech becomes systematically more complex with increasing age.

The present study investigates the relationship between age and speech complexity in children using a publicly available dataset of children's language samples. The analysis focuses on whether measures of speech complexity show systematic changes across childhood. We hypothesise that older children will produce more complex speech than younger children.

---

## Structure of the Example

The introduction follows a funnel structure:

```text
Broad topic
    ↓
Language development
    ↓
Speech complexity
    ↓
Existing research
    ↓
Specific research problem
    ↓
Research question
    ↓
Hypothesis
```

Each paragraph has a specific function.

### Paragraph 1 — Establish the field

Introduces childhood language development and explains why it matters.

### Paragraph 2 — Narrow the topic

Moves from general language development to speech complexity and identifies why a more specific measure is useful.

### Paragraph 3 — Establish the approach

Introduces spontaneous language data as a way to study speech complexity.

### Paragraph 4 — State the study

Defines the research question and hypothesis.

---

## What This Example Demonstrates

### 1. Start with the research problem

The introduction does not begin with the dataset or statistical method.

It first establishes why the research topic matters.

### 2. Narrow progressively

Each paragraph moves closer to the specific research question.

Avoid introducing unrelated literature or concepts that do not contribute to the argument.

### 3. Distinguish background from the present study

The first three paragraphs establish the context.

The final paragraph makes clear what the current study does.

### 4. State the research question explicitly

The reader should not have to infer the purpose of the study.

### 5. Keep claims proportional to evidence

The introduction does not claim that age _causes_ changes in speech complexity.

It describes an expected relationship between age and speech complexity.

## Introduction Template

A useful general template is:

```text
[Field] is important because [broad motivation].

Previous research has shown [established knowledge].
However, [limitation, uncertainty, or unresolved question].

One way to investigate this issue is [relevant method or approach].
This allows researchers to [what the approach reveals].

The present study therefore investigates [research question].
We hypothesise that [hypothesis].
```

This template should be adapted to the actual research literature rather than copied mechanically.

---

## Quality Checklist

Before finalising an introduction, ask:

- [ ] Does the introduction establish why the topic matters?
- [ ] Does it move from broad context to the specific research question?
- [ ] Does it accurately represent previous research?
- [ ] Is there a clear unresolved issue or motivation?
- [ ] Is the research question explicit?
- [ ] Are hypotheses or objectives stated where appropriate?
- [ ] Does every paragraph contribute to the argument?
- [ ] Are claims supported by appropriate evidence?
- [ ] Does the final paragraph clearly describe the present study?
- [ ] Does the introduction avoid presenting results that belong in the Results section?

---

## Key Principle

A strong introduction should make the reader understand:

**Why this question matters → what is already known → what remains unclear → what this study will investigate.**
