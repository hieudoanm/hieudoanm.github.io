# Study Design and Protocol

## Purpose

Translate a research question into a feasible, ethically appropriate, and auditable plan **before collecting or analyzing outcome data**. A protocol aligns what will be asked with who or what will be observed, how variables will be measured, and how conclusions will be drawn.

## Core principle

> Design backward from the inference the question requires; do not choose a familiar method first and retrofit the question to it.

## Workflow

1. State the question, intended inference, audience, and decision the evidence may inform.
2. Define the population or system, setting, time frame, exposure or intervention, comparator, and outcomes as relevant.
3. Review existing evidence and determine whether new data are justified.
4. Select a design that can address the question while considering feasibility and bias.
5. Define sampling, recruitment, sample-size rationale, measures, procedures, and data quality controls.
6. Specify primary outcomes, estimands, analysis, missing-data handling, exclusions, and sensitivity analyses.
7. Identify ethics, consent, privacy, safety, access, and governance requirements.
8. Register or preregister the protocol when appropriate; distinguish confirmatory plans from exploratory work.
9. Pilot procedures where useful, finalize versioned materials, and document amendments.

## Match question to design

Use the [design-selection reference](references/design-selection.md) to compare designs. Do not claim a design can support an inference it cannot identify. For causal questions, define the intervention or exposure, comparator, target population, outcome, estimand, and assumptions; observational adjustment alone does not guarantee causal identification.

## Protocol essentials

Record enough detail for review and implementation:

- Question, rationale, hypotheses, and primary estimand.
- Design, setting, eligibility, recruitment, and sample-size rationale.
- Measures, instruments, timing, procedures, and quality checks.
- Primary and secondary outcomes; analysis and decision rules.
- Missingness, exclusions, multiplicity, deviations, and sensitivity plans.
- Ethics approvals, consent, privacy, data access, and retention.

Use [assets/protocol-template.md](assets/protocol-template.md) as a starting point, not a substitute for discipline-specific protocol or registry requirements.

## Integrity safeguards

- Set primary outcomes and analyses before outcome inspection where possible.
- Record changes with dates, reasons, and whether data were already observed.
- Do not describe exploratory analyses as prespecified.
- Label assumptions and uncertainties; do not promise that a protocol eliminates bias.
- Seek qualified ethics and statistical review for consequential or specialized work.

## Completion checks

- The design and sample can answer the stated question within defined limits.
- Outcomes, measures, recruitment, and analysis are operationally specified.
- Sample-size rationale and feasibility assumptions are explicit.
- Ethics, privacy, and data-management steps have an owner and status.
- Planned versus exploratory decisions and amendment procedures are clear.
