# Preregistration

Preregistration time-stamps planned questions and methods; it does not certify quality or prevent all bias.

## Plan before outcome analysis

Specify the question, hypotheses, primary outcome, target estimand, sample and stopping rule, eligibility, exclusions, data transformations, missing-data handling, model, covariates, multiplicity, and interpretation criteria. Explain how secondary and exploratory analyses will be labeled.

## Registry and version

Choose a registry appropriate to the field, study type, and funder or journal requirements. Verify current fields and deadlines from the registry itself. Save a dated protocol version and retain confirmation or a persistent identifier.

## Deviations

Maintain a change log with date, reason, author, data-access status, affected protocol section, and analytical consequence. Distinguish changes made before versus after outcome access. Report deviations in the final output and avoid rewriting the original plan.

## Blinded analysis

Where feasible, separate data cleaning and code validation from unblinded outcome analysis. Document what was blinded, when labels were revealed, and any unavoidable departures. Preregistration improves transparency but cannot replace sound design, measurement, or reporting.
