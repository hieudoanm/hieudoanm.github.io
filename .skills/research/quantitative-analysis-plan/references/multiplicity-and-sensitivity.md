# Multiplicity and Sensitivity

## Multiplicity

Identify families of confirmatory hypotheses and the intended error-control or prioritization approach. Explain how primary, secondary, and exploratory tests differ. Select adjustment methods based on the inferential goal; a correction cannot repair poorly defined outcomes or selective reporting.

## Subgroups

Prespecify subgroup rationale, definitions, and interaction tests. Within-group significance in one subgroup and not another does not establish a difference between groups. Limit claims when subgroup sample sizes are small or categories are data-derived.

## Sensitivity analyses

Choose plausible alternatives that test key assumptions, such as missingness, outcome definition, covariate selection, model form, influential cases, or protocol deviations. Explain what each tests and how conclusions will be compared. Do not report only the version that yields the preferred conclusion.

## Equivalence and null claims

If the goal is to establish that effects are smaller than a meaningful threshold, define that threshold and use an appropriate equivalence or precision-based method. Failure to reject a null hypothesis alone does not demonstrate absence of an important effect.
