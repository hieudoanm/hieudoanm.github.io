# Protocol and Scope

## Focus the question

Use a structure suited to the question: population/intervention/comparator/outcome for intervention questions; population/index test/reference standard for diagnostic questions; exposure, outcome, and design for observational questions. Define time, setting, eligible designs, outcomes, and intended use.

## Select the review type

Choose systematic, scoping, qualitative evidence synthesis, diagnostic, prognosis, or another approach based on the objective. A broad mapping question may be better served by scoping methods; an effect question needs explicit estimands and design eligibility.

## Protocol

Specify rationale, question, eligibility, sources, draft search approach, screening process, extraction, appraisal, synthesis plan, certainty approach, reporting, and amendments. Register where appropriate and confirm current platform criteria. A protocol is a plan, not a guarantee against bias.

Define how to handle multiple reports from the same study, overlapping cohorts, multiple outcomes, language/date limits, and inaccessible full texts. Justify restrictions rather than using them by default.
