# Synthesis and Reporting

## Choose synthesis

Group studies by question, design, population, intervention/exposure, outcome, and follow-up. Explain why studies are or are not comparable. Quantitative pooling requires compatible estimands and defensible statistical assumptions; a pooled result does not erase clinical or methodological heterogeneity.

For non-pooled synthesis, use structured comparisons and explain patterns without vote counting by p-value. For qualitative evidence, use an explicit synthesis approach and retain context and contradictory cases.

## Risk of bias and certainty

Choose appraisal methods appropriate to each design and question. Distinguish risk of bias in individual studies from certainty in the body of evidence. Explain domains, judgments, and how they affect conclusions; avoid turning appraisal scores into a quality ranking without justification.

## Reporting

Use the current reporting guideline suited to review type and field. Report protocol/registration and amendments, sources and dates, selection process and flow, excluded full texts, study characteristics, appraisal, synthesis, limitations, funding/conflicts, and data availability. Follow the guideline's current instructions rather than treating a checklist as proof of methodological quality.

Conclusions should reflect uncertainty, applicability, and evidence gaps. State when conclusions are limited by sparse, indirect, or inaccessible evidence.
