# Scientific Peer Review

## Purpose

Provide a fair, evidence-based assessment that helps editors or authors judge the rigor, clarity, ethics, and contribution of a research work. Review the work, not the authors, and distinguish major validity concerns from preferences.

## Before reviewing

Confirm the deadline, criteria, confidentiality, permitted tools, and conflict-of-interest policy. Decline or disclose conflicts and scope limitations according to the venue's process. Do not upload confidential manuscripts to unapproved services or use generative tools where prohibited.

## Review workflow

1. Identify the research question, claimed contribution, design, and intended inference.
2. Check whether the methods can answer the question and whether key decisions are transparent.
3. Trace major claims to results, uncertainty, and source evidence.
4. Assess bias, measurement, analysis, ethics, data availability, and reporting proportionate to the work.
5. Identify strengths as well as limitations; distinguish fatal, major, and minor concerns.
6. Give specific, actionable recommendations and explain why each matters.
7. Provide an overall assessment that follows from the comments and venue criteria.

## Review principles

- Focus on validity, transparency, relevance, and ethical conduct—not agreement with the conclusion.
- Do not demand citations to your own work unless directly necessary.
- Avoid introducing new analyses that cannot resolve the stated question.
- Separate required changes from optional suggestions.
- Use respectful, specific language; do not speculate about author motives.
- Preserve confidentiality and submit only material you are authorized to share.

## Revisions and responses

When evaluating a revision, map each response to the original comment, inspect the actual change, and note unresolved points. Authors should answer comments directly, identify manuscript locations, explain disagreements respectfully, and report new analyses transparently.

## Completion checks

- Review follows venue instructions and confidentiality rules.
- Major comments identify the issue, evidence, consequence, and feasible remedy.
- Strengths and limitations are both represented.
- Recommendation is consistent with the substantive comments.
- Conflicts, expertise limits, and unresolved concerns are disclosed.
