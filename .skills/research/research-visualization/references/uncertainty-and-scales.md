# Uncertainty and Scales

Name what an interval represents: confidence, credible, prediction, reference, or another interval. Do not label standard errors as confidence intervals or let whiskers imply a meaning that is not defined. State the sample size and analysis unit where useful.

Show uncertainty at the level relevant to the research question, including group estimates and contrasts when appropriate. A plot of overlapping intervals is not a substitute for the planned comparison.

Use scales that reveal rather than distort. Start bars at a meaningful zero when length encodes magnitude. A truncated axis can be justified for some point or interval plots, but label it clearly and assess whether it changes perception. Disclose log, transformed, normalized, or broken scales and explain their interpretation.
