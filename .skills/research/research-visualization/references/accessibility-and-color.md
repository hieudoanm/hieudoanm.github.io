# Accessibility and Color

Use readable text, sufficient contrast, and visual encodings that do not rely on color alone. Pair color with shape, line type, pattern, or direct labels; test grayscale and common color-vision deficiencies.

Use a restrained palette with consistent meaning and avoid red/green distinctions as the only signal. Ensure labels remain legible at final print or screen size, including journal column width.

Provide meaningful alt text describing the chart's purpose, major pattern, and uncertainty without restating every value. Do not encode essential information only in interactive tooltips. Follow the target venue's file, resolution, and accessibility requirements.
