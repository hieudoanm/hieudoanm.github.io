# Research Visualization

## Purpose

Communicate a research result visually while preserving the underlying evidence, uncertainty, scale, and context. A chart is an argument about data; design choices must not imply more than the data support.

## Workflow

1. State the single question the visual should answer and who will read it.
2. Verify the source data, units, denominator, exclusions, and analysis version.
3. Choose a visual form suited to the comparison, distribution, relationship, or uncertainty.
4. Show relevant uncertainty, sample size, baseline, and context without clutter.
5. Check scales, sorting, color, labels, accessibility, and reproduction at target size.
6. Compare the visual with the underlying values and analysis.
7. Provide a caption that explains population, measure, time, and any transformation.

## Integrity rules

- Never invent observations, axes, confidence intervals, or graphical annotations.
- Avoid truncated axes or area/volume effects that exaggerate differences; disclose justified nonlinear or transformed scales.
- Show individual data or distributions where useful, not only summary bars.
- Represent uncertainty honestly and distinguish confidence intervals, standard errors, and ranges.
- Use color redundantly with shape, label, or pattern; ensure legibility in grayscale and for color-vision deficiencies.
- Clearly label illustrative or synthetic material.

## Figure and table decisions

Use tables for exact values and dense lookup; use figures for patterns, comparisons, and distributions. Avoid duplicating the same full information in both unless each serves a clear purpose. Consider direct labels and concise captions.

For graphical abstracts, do not omit critical caveats or imply a causal sequence unsupported by the design. Follow current journal specifications and retain editable source files when practical.

## Completion checks

- The visual answers a defined question and matches its source data.
- Scales, labels, units, denominators, and uncertainty are accurate.
- No visual encoding exaggerates or hides a material result.
- Accessibility and target-size readability are checked.
- Caption, provenance, software, and version are recorded.
