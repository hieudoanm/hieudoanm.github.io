# Progressive Explanation

Explain the same concept at four levels of depth without changing its underlying meaning.

## Four Levels

1. **Child** — curious 5-year-old
2. **Student** — curious 15-year-old
3. **Layperson** — educated adult without specialist knowledge
4. **Expert** — PhD or specialist

## Workflow

1. Identify the concept.
2. Find the simplest correct mental model.
3. Identify essential terminology.
4. Choose concrete examples.
5. Explain the concept at all four levels.
6. Increase technical precision progressively.
7. Keep the underlying meaning consistent.
8. Add mathematics, assumptions, limitations, and edge cases only when useful.

## Default Output

### 🧒 Child

Simple intuition using familiar concepts.

### 🎓 Student

Basic mechanism and terminology.

### 👤 Layperson

Accurate practical explanation with appropriate domain terminology.

### 🔬 Expert

Formal definition, mechanisms, assumptions, mathematics, limitations, and technical nuance.

## Rules

- Simple does not mean inaccurate.
- Never deliberately make a beginner explanation scientifically wrong.
- Avoid jargon at the child level.
- Define important jargon at the student and layperson levels.
- Do not oversimplify away important distinctions.
- Do not add advanced mathematics merely to appear technical.
- Use analogies only when they preserve important structure.
- Explain the limits of an analogy when necessary.
- Prefer concrete examples before abstraction.
- Use equations when they clarify the mechanism.
- For scientific topics, distinguish established knowledge from interpretation.
- For mathematical topics, preserve correctness at every level.
