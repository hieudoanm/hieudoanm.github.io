# Explanation Levels

The skill uses four audiences representing increasing knowledge,
abstraction, technical vocabulary, and mathematical sophistication.

## 1. Child

**Target:** Curious 5-year-old.

### Assume

- Everyday knowledge.
- Limited formal vocabulary.
- Learns through concrete examples.
- No specialist terminology.

### Use

- Everyday language.
- Concrete objects.
- Simple cause and effect.
- Short sentences.
- Familiar analogies.
- Simple questions.

### Avoid

- Specialist terminology.
- Equations.
- Abstract definitions.
- Unexplained technical words.

### Goal

Create a correct intuitive mental model.

Answer:

> What is this, in a way I can imagine?

---

## 2. Student

**Target:** Curious 15-year-old.

### Assume

- General school-level science and mathematics.
- Basic abstraction.
- Ability to learn terminology.
- Ability to follow simple equations.

### Use

- Basic technical vocabulary.
- Concrete examples.
- Cause-and-effect reasoning.
- Simple mathematical notation.
- Comparisons with familiar concepts.

### Goal

Build foundational understanding.

Answer:

> How does this work, and why does it matter?

---

## 3. Layperson

**Target:** Educated adult without specialist knowledge.

### Assume

- General literacy.
- Basic mathematics.
- Ability to understand abstract ideas.
- No domain-specific training.

### Use

- Correct domain terminology.
- Formal definitions where useful.
- Practical examples.
- Real-world applications.
- Important limitations.
- Moderate technical detail.

### Avoid

- Specialist assumptions.
- Excessive notation.
- Unnecessary implementation details.

### Goal

Give technically accurate understanding without requiring specialist training.

Answer:

> What does this mean, how is it used, and what are its limitations?

---

## 4. Expert

**Target:** PhD, researcher, or domain specialist.

### Assume

- Strong domain knowledge.
- Familiarity with technical terminology.
- Ability to interpret equations.
- Understanding of standard methods.

### Use

- Precise terminology.
- Formal definitions.
- Mathematical notation.
- Mechanistic explanations.
- Assumptions.
- Edge cases.
- Competing interpretations.
- Methodological limitations.
- Relevant theoretical distinctions.

### Avoid

- Explaining basic terminology unnecessarily.
- Replacing technical precision with metaphors.
- Adding complexity without informational value.

### Goal

Provide technically rigorous understanding.

Answer:

> What is the precise formulation, how does it work mathematically,
> what assumptions does it make, and where does it break down?

## Progression

```text
Concrete
   ↓
Intuitive
   ↓
Conceptual
   ↓
Formal
   ↓
Technical
```

Complexity should increase primarily through abstraction and precision,
not unnecessary verbosity.
