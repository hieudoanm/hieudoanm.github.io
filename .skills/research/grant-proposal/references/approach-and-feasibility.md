# Approach and Feasibility

Describe enough method for reviewers to judge validity, rigor, and feasibility. Align design, sample, measures, analysis, milestones, ethics, data governance, and team expertise with each aim.

Discuss foreseeable risks and alternatives, including recruitment, measurement, access, technical failure, null findings, and dependencies. A contingency should preserve the scientific question where possible rather than silently changing the outcome.

Support feasibility with verified preliminary data, prior work, letters, access agreements, or pilot evidence. Distinguish completed evidence from planned work. Include expertise and collaborators with explicit roles; do not imply commitments that are not confirmed.

Use a realistic timeline with decision gates and dependencies. Check that scope and resources fit the award period and any institutional constraints.
