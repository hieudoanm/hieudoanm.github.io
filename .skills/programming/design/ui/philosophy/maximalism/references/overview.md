# Maximalism

Four philosophies in this directory form one map. Minimalism and maximalism vary
**how much** is present; flat and brutalism vary **how finished** it looks.

```text
                      polished                     raw
   less               minimalism                  brutalist minimalism
   more               maximalism                  brutalist maximalism
```

Maximalism is **deliberate density and expression in service of the content.**
Where minimalism says _remove until the structure is clear_, maximalism says
**add until the structure is felt** — pattern, saturation, layering, ceremony,
scale. Density is the gift it offers the reader who wants everything.

**This is not "more stuff."** An over-decorated page with no hierarchy is noise,
not maximalism. The difference is whether the additions were _governed by a
rule_. Maximalism is ornament deployed as grammar; chaos is ornament deployed as
panic.

**Roots:** Baroque and Rococo interiors (ornament as total environment), Arts and
Crafts and Gujarati/Kutch embroidery (repetition as craft), psychedelic poster
work (Wes Wilson, Rick Griffin), the Memphis Group (Sottsass, 1981 —
maximalism as deliberate anti-corporate play), illuminated manuscripts and their
crowded marginalia, Mexican muralism, Art Nouveau, and Afrofuturist visual
culture. Contrast `design/philosophy/brutalism.md` for the same impulse without
the finish.

## 1. Core Principles

- **Ornament is structure** — decoration that repeats becomes a visual grammar.
  Filler decoration communicates nothing; patterned space communicates energy,
  place, and era.
- **Systematic or nothing** — repetition plus alignment. The same motif applied
  consistently reads as language; applied randomly it reads as an error.
- **Density as generosity** — a catalogue that shows everything rewards the reader
  who wants it. Hiding is the minimalism failure mode, and sometimes it is right.
- **Scale contrast is the engine** — drama comes from extreme size differences
  between elements. A maximalist layout with uniform sizes is merely busy.
- **Ceremony over recede** — symmetrical, frontal, layered composition. The page
  performs; it does not step aside.
- **Every surface earns pattern** — whitespace can itself be patterned. "Empty"
  is a choice, not a default.
- **The reader must still be able to find things** — see §2. This is the hard
  constraint, not an afterthought.

---

## 2. The One Non-Negotiable: Hierarchy

Maximalism fails in exactly one way that matters: **density without a reading
order becomes noise.** Before adding anything, fix the order the eye moves in.

- **One focal point, unmistakably.** The most saturated, largest, or most
  detailed element on the screen. Exactly one.
- **Three levels, as always** — focal, supporting, ambient. Density is permitted
  between levels; ambiguity is not.
- **If two elements compete, the composition is broken** — not the audience's
  problem to solve.

Ask before applying maximalism: _is this added because the content needs it, or
because a decision about hierarchy was avoided?_ The second is decoration as
avoidance, which is the most common misuse.
