# Workflow: Research

## Trigger

The task requires finding, evaluating, synthesizing, reproducing, replicating, or communicating research evidence.

## Goal

Produce a transparent, traceable research artifact whose claims are proportionate to the evidence and whose methods can be inspected or repeated.

These routes are entry points, not a mandatory linear pipeline. Pair study design with an analysis plan when quantitative inference is intended, and establish data governance before collection or access. A systematic review uses explicit, reproducible identification and selection methods; a literature review is the broader synthesis route, and meta-analysis is only a statistical method within a defensible study set. Combine skills only when needed; distinguish reproduction (same data/methods) from replication (new evidence). Choose qualitative analysis for primary qualitative material and a suitable qualitative evidence synthesis for published studies.

## Steps

1. **Frame the question** — State the question, audience, intended artifact, and scope. Record what is known, uncertain, and out of scope.
2. **Select methods** — Read the selected skill and relevant references. Define search sources, eligibility, analysis, or transformation rules before applying them.
3. **Track provenance** — Record sources, dates, selection/exclusion decisions, versions, and transformations. Separate observed evidence from interpretation and illustrative material.
4. **Assess quality** — Check methods, bias, missing data, uncertainty, and alternative explanations appropriate to the discipline and design.
5. **Synthesize proportionately** — Compare findings and methods; do not infer causation from association or claim consensus from incomplete coverage.
6. **Verify the artifact** — Check citations against sources, arithmetic and analysis against recorded inputs, and figures/tables against underlying data. Label placeholders or synthetic examples visibly.
7. **Report** — Summarize the question, method, main evidence, limitations, and unresolved uncertainties.

## Definition of Done

- [ ] The research question, scope, and intended output are explicit.
- [ ] Methods and source provenance are recorded well enough to inspect or repeat.
- [ ] Claims are traceable and no stronger than the evidence supports.
- [ ] Missing evidence, limitations, and uncertainty are visible.
- [ ] Illustrative or synthetic data are clearly labeled and cannot be mistaken for findings.
- [ ] The selected skill's validation guidance and applicable reporting requirements are followed.
