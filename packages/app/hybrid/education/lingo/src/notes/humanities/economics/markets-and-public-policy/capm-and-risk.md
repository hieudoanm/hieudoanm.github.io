## What is it?

The **Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)** describes the relationship between
the risk of an asset and its expected return. Its core insight: investors are
only rewarded for **systematic risk**—the market-wide risk that cannot be
diversified away—not for the asset-specific risk that a diversified portfolio
eliminates. Return is measured by the asset’s **beta**.

## The key concept: beta

**Systematic vs specific risk:** Some risk moves with the whole market
(recessions, rate changes); other risk is unique to one company (a bad product
launch). Diversification washes out the specific risk, leaving only systematic
risk.

**Beta:** Measures an asset’s sensitivity to the market. A beta of 1 moves with
the market; above 1 is more volatile, below 1 is calmer. High-beta assets must
offer higher expected returns.

**The SML:** The Security Market Line plots expected return against beta—the
pricing relationship at the heart of CAPM.

## The formula

**Expected return = Risk-free rate + beta × (Market return − Risk-free rate).**
The first term rewards waiting; the second rewards bearing systematic risk.

**The risk premium:** The gap between the market’s expected return and the
risk-free rate is the price of bearing market risk overall, scaled by each
asset’s beta.

**Risk-free proxy:** Government bond yields are typically used as the risk-free
rate, standing in for a return with no default risk.

## Uses and limits

**Cost of equity:** Firms and investors use CAPM to estimate the required return
on equity and to discount project cash flows.

**Simplicity tradeoff:** CAPM reduces risk to a single number (beta); reality is
richer, and models like the Fama-French factors add size and value as additional
priced risks.

**Estimation challenges:** Betas and the market risk premium are estimated from
history and can be unstable, so CAPM’s outputs are useful guides rather than
precise prices.
