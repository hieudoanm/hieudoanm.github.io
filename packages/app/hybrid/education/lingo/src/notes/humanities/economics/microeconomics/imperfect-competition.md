# Imperfect Competition

> The real world lies between perfect competition and monopoly—where firms hold
> some pricing power.

App route: `/economics/imperfect-competition/`

## What is it?

**Imperfect competition** describes markets where firms hold some degree of
market power, so they face downward-sloping demand curves and can raise price
above marginal cost. It spans **monopolistic competition** (many firms selling
differentiated products) and **oligopoly** (a few large firms). Almost every
real market is imperfectly competitive—perfect competition is an idealized
benchmark.

## Monopolistic competition

**Many firms, differentiated products:** Restaurants, clothing brands, and local
firms compete vigorously while each offers something slightly unique—a distinct
brand, quality, or location.

**Some pricing power:** Differentiation means each firm can raise price a little
without losing all customers, unlike a competitive price taker.

**Free entry and zero long-run profit:** Profit attracts entrants who erode it
until, in the long run, firms earn only normal profit—but they still price above
marginal cost, leaving excess capacity relative to the ideal.

## The spectrum of market structures

**Perfect competition:** Many identical producers, no market power, price equals
marginal cost.

**Monopolistic competition:** Many differentiated firms with modest market
power.

**Oligopoly:** A few large firms whose decisions are interdependent, with
significant market power (see Oligopoly).

**Monopoly:** A single seller with maximum market power (see Monopoly & Market
Power).

## Why it matters

**Higher prices, more variety:** Market power lets firms price above cost (a
deadweight loss), but differentiation delivers product variety consumers value—a
genuine tradeoff.

**Advertising and branding:** Under imperfect competition, firms invest heavily
in advertising to differentiate and protect market share, which can be
informative or wasteful.

**Antitrust:** Regulators care about how much market power firms hold and
exercise, scrutinizing mergers that would reduce competition.

**Most markets:** From coffee shops to airlines, understanding imperfect
competition is key to real-world pricing and firm strategy.
