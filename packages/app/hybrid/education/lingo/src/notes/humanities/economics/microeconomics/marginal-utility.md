## What is it?

**Marginal utility** is the additional satisfaction gained from consuming one
more unit of a good. The central idea, the **law of diminishing marginal
utility**, is that each additional unit provides less satisfaction than the one
before it. That is why the first slice of pizza is delicious and the fifth is a
struggle. It underlies the shape of demand curves and how people allocate their
budgets.

## The core concept

**Total vs marginal:** Total utility is the overall satisfaction from all units
consumed. Marginal utility is the change from the next one—it is the “marginal”
in “one more.”

**Diminishing returns:** Early units deliver the most gain; later units less.
This does not mean later units are bad—just that each adds less than the last.

**Rational stopping point:** A person keeps consuming as long as the next unit’s
marginal utility exceeds its cost, stopping where marginal benefit equals
marginal cost.

## Budget allocation

**Equalizing margins:** To get the most satisfaction from a fixed budget, spend
each dollar where its marginal utility per dollar is highest—until the last
dollar spent on each good yields equal marginal utility per dollar.

**Why it links to demand:** Because marginal utility falls as consumption rises,
buyers are only willing to pay less for additional units—directly producing the
downward-sloping demand curve.

**The paradox of value:** Water is vital yet cheap, while diamonds are frivolous
yet expensive. Water has high total utility but low marginal utility (it is
abundant); diamonds the reverse. Price tracks marginal, not total, value.

## Real-world applications

**Pricing and bundles:** Businesses set the price of the next unit by what it is
worth to a marginal customer—and package deals capture surplus by charging more
for early units and less for later ones.

**Public policy:** Progressive taxation is justified by diminishing marginal
utility of income—the 100th dollar of income buys less satisfaction than the
10th, so taking more from the rich costs them less wellbeing per dollar.

**Everyday decisions:** Choosing between “one more hour of work” (marginal wage)
and “one more hour of leisure” (marginal utility of rest) is a marginal-utility
tradeoff we all make.
