# Bargaining Theory

> How two parties divide a surplus—and how the rules of negotiation decide the
> split.

App route: `/economics/bargaining-theory/`

## What is it?

**Bargaining theory** studies how parties divide a surplus (money, power, gains
from trade) when there is both common interest and conflict. Both sides prefer a
deal to nothing, but each wants the larger share. The outcome depends on
alternatives, patience, and the rules of the negotiation. John Nash’s axiomatic
approach and Rubinstein’s alternating-offer model are the classic frameworks.

## Nash bargaining solution

**The framework:** Nash (1950) asked what a fair, efficient bargain between two
parties would look like, deriving a solution from axioms of symmetry,
efficiency, and independence.

**Maximize the product of gains:** The solution splits the surplus to maximize
the product of each side’s gain over its **disagreement point** (what each gets
if no deal happens).

**Threat points matter:** A party with a better outside option walks away with
more. The better your fallback, the bigger your share of the surplus.

## Rubinstein bargaining

**Alternating offers:** Players take turns proposing a split, and bargaining
continues across rounds. Each round costs something because the surplus shrinks
over time.

**Patience is power:** The more patient player—who discounts the future less—can
hold out longer and capture a larger share, because the impatient player
concedes to avoid delay.

**Immediate agreement:** In equilibrium, a deal is reached on the first round
with an efficient split reflecting the relative patience of the two sides.

## Real-world applications

**Wage negotiations:** A worker’s leverage in salary talks depends on outside
offers and how quickly each side wants to settle.

**Deal-making:** Mergers, settlements, and two-party deals follow bargaining
logic: alternatives and patience determine who captures the gains.

**International disputes:** Dividing territory, resources, or treaty concessions
reflects the relative power and patience of the negotiating parties.

**Bargaining power:** Improving your alternatives (building skills, cultivating
options) is the practical lever bargaining theory hands to negotiators.
