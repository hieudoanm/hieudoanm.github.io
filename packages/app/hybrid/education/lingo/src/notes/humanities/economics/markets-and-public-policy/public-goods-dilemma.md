## What is it?

A **public good** is something that is both **non-excludable** (you can’t stop
people from using it) and **non-rivalrous** (one person’s use doesn’t reduce
availability for others). Because no one can be excluded, individuals have an
incentive to **free-ride**—benefit from the good without paying for it. When too
many people free-ride, the good is under-provided or fails to exist at all. That
is the public goods dilemma.

## Core properties

**Non-excludable:** Once provided, it is practically impossible to exclude
anyone from benefiting. National defense protects everyone in the country,
whether or not they pay taxes.

**Non-rivalrous:** One person’s consumption does not diminish what is available
for others. Your listening to a radio broadcast doesn’t prevent anyone else from
listening.

**The free-rider problem:** Since nobody can be charged for using the good,
markets have little incentive to supply it, and voluntary contribution typically
falls short of the socially optimal level.

## Real-world examples

**Clean air:** Everyone benefits, but no individual is personally rewarded for
reducing pollution.

**Public broadcasting and open-source software:** Rely on voluntary donations
and contributions even though millions use them free of charge.

**Lighthouses and streetlights:** Help every passing ship or pedestrian
regardless of who paid.

**Scientific knowledge:** Research findings benefit everyone once published,
which is why public funding and open-access publishing matter.

## The solutions

**Government provision through taxation:** The clearest fix—everyone pays taxes,
and the state supplies the good so it is funded even if no one would volunteer.

**Compulsory membership:** Groups make funding mandatory (association dues,
mandatory contributions) to prevent free-riding within the group.

**Exclusion mechanisms:** Find ways to make the good partly excludable, such as
subscription tiers for otherwise public digital content.

**Reputation and altruism:** Recognition, social norms, and warm-glow giving
motivate enough voluntary contribution to sustain open-source projects and
crowdfunded goods.
