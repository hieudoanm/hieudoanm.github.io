# The IS-LM Model

> A classic framework for how goods markets and money markets interact to set
> output and rates.

App route: `/economics/is-lm-model/`

## What is it?

The **IS-LM model**, developed by John Hicks and Alvin Hansen, captures the
interaction between the **goods market** (the **IS** curve: investment-saving)
and the **money market** (the **LM** curve: liquidity preference-money supply).
Their intersection determines short-run output and the interest rate. It is the
workhorse framework for teaching how fiscal and monetary policy affect the
economy.

## The IS curve

**Goods market equilibrium:** The IS curve pairs interest rates with output
where planned spending equals income (saving equals investment).

**Downward sloping:** Lower interest rates make borrowing cheaper, stimulating
investment and output—so each interest rate maps to a higher level of output.

**Shifts:** Expansionary fiscal policy (more spending or lower taxes) shifts IS
outward, raising output at every rate.

## The LM curve

**Money market equilibrium:** The LM curve pairs interest rates with output
where money demand equals the money supply.

**Upward sloping:** Higher output raises the demand for money (for
transactions), pushing up the interest rate that clears the money market.

**Shifts:** Expansionary monetary policy—increasing the money supply—shifts LM
outward, lowering rates at every level of output.

## Applying the model

**Equilibrium:** The IS and LM curves cross at one (interest rate, output) pair
where both markets clear simultaneously.

**Fiscal policy:** Shifting IS raises output but also raises interest rates,
which partially “crowds out” investment.

**Monetary policy:** Shifting LM lowers rates and raises output, illustrating
the transmission of policy through interest rates.

**Limitations:** The model uses fixed prices and focuses on the short run; it is
a teaching tool more than a complete description of modern economies.
