## What is it?

The **time value of money (TVM)** is the principle that a sum of money has
different value depending on when it is received. A dollar today is worth more
than a dollar tomorrow because it can be invested to earn interest, or spent
now, while future dollars carry risk and suffer inflation. This idea underpins
interest rates, loans, investments, and valuation across all of finance.

## Present and future value

**Future value:** How much a sum grows when invested at an interest rate. It is
the present amount compounded forward: FV = PV × (1 + r)<sup>t</sup>.

**Present value:** How much a future sum is worth today. It discounts the future
amount back: PV = FV / (1 + r) <sup>t</sup>.

**Discounting:** The higher the interest rate, the less a future dollar is worth
today. Discounting turns any stream of future payments into a comparable
present-day number.

## Compounding and discounting

**Compounding:** Interest earned on interest grows money exponentially. The
earlier you start saving, the more compounding multiplies your balance—why
starting young matters so much.

**Annuities and perpetuities:** A fixed payment each period has a present value
computed by discounting the whole stream—the basis of mortgages, pensions, and
bond pricing.

**The rule of 72:** Roughly, doubling time in years equals 72 divided by the
annual return, giving a quick intuition for compounding.

## Why it matters

**Investment decisions:** Comparing a project’s future cash flows to its cost
today requires discounting them all to present value (net present value
analysis).

**Bonds and loans:** Bond prices and loan terms are determined by discounting
promised future payments at the market interest rate.

**Retirement planning:** Saving today relies on knowing how much a stream of
future contributions will be worth.

**Inflation and risk:** Delayed money must compensate for lost purchasing power
and uncertainty, which is why interest rates embed an inflation premium and a
risk premium.
