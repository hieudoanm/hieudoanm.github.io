## Relative luminance

Contrast ratio compares how bright two colors appear to the eye. The eye does
not weight red, green and blue equally — green contributes most, blue least — so
**relative luminance** applies those weights before comparing.

WCAG sets pass thresholds: 4.5:1 for normal text (AA), 3:1 for large text, and
7:1 (AAA) on normal text. The same checks apply to icons and graphical elements,
not only text.

## Color vision deficiency

Common deficiencies come from missing cones in the retina. Protanopia lacks red
cones, deuteranopia lacks green cones, and tritanopia lacks blue cones. Pairs
that look different to a typical viewer can collapse for someone affected.

The practical rule: never rely on hue alone to convey meaning — pair it with
position, shape or text.

## Warm and cool

Physical light temperature is measured in Kelvin: candlelight (~2000K) is warm
and orange, daylight (~6500K) is white, shade and sky (~10000K) are cool and
blue.

In color theory the same words describe hue position: reds, oranges and yellows
feel warm; greens, blues and violets feel cool. Low-saturation colors read as
neutral.
