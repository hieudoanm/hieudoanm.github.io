## Why writing helps

Writing is a **cognitive-emotional rehearsal**. Verbalising an experience moves
it from implicit, fast, emotional processing into explicit, slower,
language-based processing, which is associated with weaker emotional reactivity
to the memory afterwards.

It also externalises working memory. Offloading a worry onto a page frees the
limited-capacity store for other tasks — the effect people report as “getting it
out of my head”.

Because writing is slow, it also **interferes with rumination**. Undisturbed
recall lets a mood reinforce itself; putting a sequence of events in order
forces the alternative of narrative, which requires facts, causes and a next
step.

## Expressive versus analytic writing

The two dominant forms produce different effects. **Expressive writing** is
descriptive and unstructured: what happened, how it felt, without editing or
interpretation. **Analytic writing** states a claim and then tests it against
evidence.

Trials comparing them find the expressive form is associated with reduced
intrusion of traumatic memories, while the analytic form is associated with
improved marks and with greater insight into an illness. The mechanism differs:
one regulates emotion, the other produces understanding.

Structured templates generally outperform free-form writing when the goal is
specific — mood tracking, a gratitude column, or a list of what is within and
outside one&apos;s control.

## A workable routine

Fifteen minutes most days beats an occasional long session. Two minutes — one
page, unedited — is enough to start, and the consistency is what produces the
effect.

The most useful prompt is usually not open-ended. Something like “what is taking
up the most space today, and what would one small step be” turns a mood into a
decision. Writing to an imagined reader is also a standard technique for
building empathy: it forces you to supply what the other person knows that you
do not.

One caution: mood-tracking scales can become a source of rumination if used only
to record distress. Pair tracking with something generative, and treat a bad run
as data rather than a verdict.
