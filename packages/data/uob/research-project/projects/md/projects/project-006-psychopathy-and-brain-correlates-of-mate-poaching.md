# Psychopathy and brain correlates of mate poaching

## Project title

Psychopathy and brain correlates of mate poaching

## Project summary

This project is a direct collaboration with colleagues at Kyoto University in Japan; regular contact with them will be necessary.

Purpose, background, and research questions

This research project explores how personality traits relate to behaviours around romantic attraction, specifically in situations where someone tries to attract a person who is already in a relationship — a behaviour known as "mate-poaching". While this behaviour is generally considered socially unacceptable, it happens fairly often in many cultures and may reflect different personal motivations.

The main part of the project will be a brain imaging (MRI) study with young adult men in the UK to investigate whether differences in brain activity — particularly in areas involved in decision-making and how we evaluate others — relate to people’s willingness to engage in mate-poaching. We are especially interested in whether brain responses explain the link between certain personality traits and this type of behaviour.

Hypotheses / research questions:

Which aspects of personality are most strongly linked to a tendency to pursue others who are already in relationships?

Are specific areas of brain activity or brain structures/network, especially in regions linked to evaluating others and making decisions, associated with interest in mate-poaching?

Can differences in brain responses or brain structure help explain why some men with higher levels of certain personality traits are more likely to engage in this behaviour?

Expected outcomes:

We expect to find that men who score higher on certain personality traits (e.g., impulsiveness, emotional coldness) report more frequent mate-poaching behaviour. We expect that men who show more interest in mate-poaching will also show stronger or more distinct brain activity in specific regions involved in decision-making. These brain patterns may help explain why some people are more likely to engage in this type of behaviour.
