## Project title

Seeing Is Not Enough: How Active Exploration Shapes the Perception of Virtual Materials

## Project summary

People normally judge material properties by actively moving, looking and touching. In virtual reality, however, material perception is frequently based on visual information alone. This project will test whether controlling one's own viewpoint or hand movement improves judgements of virtual material properties. Participants will compare virtual objects that differ in apparent softness, elasticity or weight. In an active condition, participants will explore each object through their own movements; in a passive condition, they will observe matched prerecorded movement. Accuracy, discrimination thresholds, confidence and exploration behaviour will be measured. The project requires standard VR Lab equipment but no specialist haptic device. It contributes directly to ongoing VR Lab and EXPLORA research on active perception of materials, objects and spaces. A supported psychophysical experiment will be suitable for Psychology Conversion students, with an optional movement-analysis extension for students with computational interests. The project may contribute to a preregistered open dataset and a subsequent publication.

## Topic

Active perception; virtual materials; psychophysics; multisensory perception; visual-haptic interaction; virtual reality; movement

## Methodology

Quantitative within-participant psychophysical VR experiment comparing active exploration with matched passive observation. Participants will make forced-choice judgements about one clearly defined virtual material property, such as compliance or elasticity. Primary outcomes will be discrimination accuracy and/or psychometric thresholds; secondary outcomes will include confidence, response time and exploration trajectories. Sample size will be determined by an a priori power analysis. Repeated-measures or mixed-effects models and psychometric-function fitting will test the effect of exploration condition. The core study will use existing equipment and be scoped for completion of practical work by May.
