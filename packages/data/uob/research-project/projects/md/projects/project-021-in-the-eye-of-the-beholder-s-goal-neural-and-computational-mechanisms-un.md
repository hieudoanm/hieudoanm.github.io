## Project title

In the eye of the beholder's goal: neural and computational mechanisms underpinning value-based choice

## Project summary

From the choice of breakfast to the choice of romantic partner, value-based decisions permeate everyday life. Research has shown that these decisions are linked to people's attention: Options that are looked at more are more likely to be chosen. This robust empirical result has been taken as evidence that attention amplifies the value of options. However, more recent work suggests that the relationship is the other way around: The more people consider choosing an option, the more they will look at it, regardless of its value. Disrupting this choice-attention feedback loop reveals a separate, distinct effect of attention on choice that depends on people's expectations and how an options' value compares to them. In addition, research has show that people's choice behavior and gaze patterns are strongly influenced not by value per se, but by how values relate to the decision-maker's current goal. Taken together this work suggests that goals and attention jointly shape how people evaluate their options and ultimately choose. Here we will use attention and goal manipulations to test predictions of a model of this interaction and record EEG to shed new light on the underlying neural correlated of value-processing and decision-making.
