## Project title

What Makes AI-Generated Faces Look Real? Investigating the Role of Spatial Frequency in Human Perception

## Project summary

AI-generated faces have become remarkably realistic. People now struggle to distinguish AI-generated faces from photographs of real people and, strikingly, AI-generated faces can sometimes be perceived as more real than genuine faces—a phenomenon known as AI hyper-realism. But what visual information leads us to perceive an AI-generated face as real?
This project will investigate one potential mechanism: spatial-frequency information. The human visual system processes images at different spatial frequencies, which capture different levels of visual detail. Face recognition relies particularly strongly on information within certain spatial-frequency ranges. Interestingly, AI-generated images can contain subtle abnormalities in the distribution of information across these frequencies. Machine-learning systems can exploit these frequency-domain signatures to distinguish real from AI-generated images, but it remains unclear whether they also influence human perceptions of facial realness.

The project will investigate whether spatial-frequency characteristics associated with AI-generated faces help explain why some artificial faces appear so convincing. First, the student will conduct image analyses comparing the spatial-frequency properties of real and AI-generated faces. These findings will then inform manipulations of real facial images, allowing us to experimentally alter their spatial-frequency properties to more closely resemble those found in AI-generated faces. In an online behavioural experiment, participants will judge whether faces are real or AI-generated, allowing us to test whether manipulating this visual information systematically changes perceptions of realness.

The student will join an established research project and team of researchers (including Kyra Scott (https://www.appliedmemorylab.co.uk/kyra-scott), Prof Howard Bowman, and external collaborators) and will work with an existing experimental paradigm; all programming required for the online experiment has already been completed. The project therefore provides an opportunity to develop skills in face perception, experimental psychology, image analysis, online behavioural research and quantitative data analysis, while addressing a timely question at the intersection of human perception and artificial intelligence. It is anticipated that this work will be published.
