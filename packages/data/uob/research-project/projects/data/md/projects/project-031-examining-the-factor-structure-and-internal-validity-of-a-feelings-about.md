## Project title

Examining the factor structure and internal validity of a feelings about literacy scale

## Project summary

In our highly literate society, being good at reading is essential for thriving in education and employment. Key to developing these reading skills is regular leisure time reading (Pfost et al., 2018). However, reports on adult and young people’s reading show leisure time reading has declined over the years. According to theory on reading engagement, founded in principles of self-determination theory (SDT, Ryan & Deci, 2000), development of reading motivation hinges on intrinsic and extrinsic factors (e.g. Conradi et al., 2014). It is also argued it’s not only our actual reading ability that is associated with how much we engage in reading and writing in our own time, but how we feel about our own reading and writing skills (e.g. our perceived competence) also determines whether we actually engage with these activities.
The purpose of the project is to explore the relationship between feelings about reading and writing, and whether this is associated with the amount of recreational reading and writing that adults engage in. This project aims to validate the factor structure and internal validity of a feelings about literacy scale, developed by adapting several previously published scales (i.e. Reading amount, adapted from Schutte & Malouff, 2007, affect, adapted from MacArthur et al. 2015, and self-efficacy, adapted from Carroll & Fox 2017; and writing amount, adapted from Schutte & Malouff, 2007, affect, adapted from MacArthur et al. 2015, self-efficacy, adapted from MacArthur et al. 2015).
The existing data set includes data from ~600 undergraduate students and includes data about demographic factors (e.g., age, gender, student status) and reading and writing frequency and self-competence.
