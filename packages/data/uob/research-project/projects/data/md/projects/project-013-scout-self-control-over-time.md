## Project title

SCOUT - Self-Control Over Time

## Project summary

SCOUT is a longitudinal observational study examining how students' self-control changes across academic demands and recovery, and how those changes relate to stress, sleep, activity and other everyday experiences. Participants complete five monthly questionnaire sessions and, where available, provide weekly summary values from an eligible Apple Watch, Garmin or Fitbit during three brief researcher-assisted sessions. MSc students will develop a focused question within this wider programme and analyse repeated-measures data under close supervision. Possible topics include within-person links between stress and self-control; sleep or activity as predictors of month-to-month change; differences between stable individual characteristics and time-varying states; and the effect of missing wearable data on conclusions. The default MSc route uses a fixed, fully anonymised analysis dataset prepared by the supervisor. Each student will assist with prospective data collection. The project develops skills in longitudinal design, reproducible analysis, cautious interpretation and open-science reporting.

## Topic

Self-control; stress; sleep; physical activity; wearable data; longitudinal research; repeated measures; student wellbeing; academic demands; multilevel modelling; reproducible research

## Methodology

Quantitative longitudinal observational research. Students will analyse repeated self-report and weekly wearable-summary data ideally using R or another language. The core approach will use mixed-effects models to examine change over time and associations between stress, self-control, sleep and activity, while distinguishing within-person change from between-person differences. Students will document scale scoring, missingness, attrition and analytical decisions in a reproducible workflow.
