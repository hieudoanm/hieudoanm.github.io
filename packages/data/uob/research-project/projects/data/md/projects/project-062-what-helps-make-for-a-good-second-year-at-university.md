## Project title

What helps make for a good second year at University?

## Project summary

Much of the institutional efforts in supporting UK undergraduate students are focused on the first year of study with its transition to university (with retention an important metric) and on the final year of study in which students complete their individual projects and are getting ready for the next step in their careers (with employment statistics and the National Student Survey – NSS as measures of success for the institution). The second year of study, however, brings its own challenges: there is a step up in academic demands, grades start to count towards the final degree classification, and many students will be living in shared houses with their friends for the first time. There is documented evidence of ‘second year blues’ in the UK (e.g. Jacobs & Newstead, 2000), and of an equivalent ‘sophomore slump’ in the US (e.g. Pattengale & Schreiner, 2000). Yorke (2015) argues that looking after students in the middle of their programmes is important in enhancing student experience and success in a book about second year student experiences published by the Society for Research in Higher Education.

The aim of this project is to explore the experiences of second year students in Higher Education with a view to understanding the specific challenges faced by students in the ‘forgotten year’ and going forward to develop resources to support the transition to and experience of the second year on undergraduate programmes. There will be scope for each student to personalise their project. For example, you could on specific aspects of student experience (e.g. assessment and feedback) or take a more holistic approach.
