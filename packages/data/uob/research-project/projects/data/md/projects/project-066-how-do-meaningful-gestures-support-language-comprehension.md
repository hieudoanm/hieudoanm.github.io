## Project title

How do meaningful gestures support language comprehension?

## Project summary

Successful communication requires us to rapidly select and interpret words from among the thousands of words represented in our brains. This is a remarkable computational challenge that may be affected by increasing experience with more than one language. One way the brain makes comprehension more efficient is by using context to anticipate upcoming information. In face-to-face communication, that context comes not only from speech, but also from what we see: a speaker's facial movements and gestures can help us understand what is being said and may even allow us to predict what is coming next. This project will examine what meanings people assign to gestures (e.g., strumming gesture), how meaningful gestures contribute to prediction of specific words when combined with speech, and how meaningful gestures affect the processing and comprehension of subsequent information. We will also ask whether these effects vary across the adult lifespan and as a function of bilingual experience. The student will contribute to an ongoing programme of research examining how visual information and speech input work together to support efficient communication.

## Topic

Multimodal language comprehension; gesture; speech; language prediction; bilingualism; ageing; individual differences;

## Methodology

Online and/or in person experiments using videos of a virtual human making gestures. Participants will be asked about gesture meanings, complete sentence fragments presented with and without gesture, and provide measures of bilingual experience and cognitive control. Data will be analysed in R using response probabilities, informativeness measures, and mixed-effects models where appropriate.
