## Project title

From Papers to Evidence: Can AI Build a Reliable Research Vault?

## Project summary

Research databases such as Locomotion Vault and the Text Entry for XR Trove demonstrate how structured collections of publications and techniques can reveal patterns that are difficult to identify through conventional narrative reviews. However, constructing these databases manually requires substantial expert time. This project will develop and evaluate an AI-assisted tool that identifies publications within a focused area of XR research, initially virtual-hand and embodied interaction, and converts them into a structured, traceable database. The tool will extract bibliographic information, study characteristics, participant numbers, interaction techniques, experimental conditions, outcome measures and numerical results. Every extracted value must be linked to supporting text or tables in the original publication. A manually coded subset will provide a gold-standard benchmark. The student will measure retrieval recall, extraction precision, accuracy, hallucination rate, processing cost and time saved. After validation, the resulting dataset will be used to test a preregistered research hypothesis, such as whether embodied hand input or multimodal feedback predicts improved performance, workload or user experience in XR. The central question is whether AI output is sufficiently accurate, reproducible and transparent to support valid scientific inference.

## Topic

Artificial intelligence; data science; large language models; scientific information extraction; literature mining; evidence synthesis; research databases; extended reality; reproducible research

## Methodology

Quantitative computational project. First, the student will define a focused XR domain, inclusion criteria and structured extraction schema. A reproducible pipeline will then discover publications, deduplicate records, screen them for eligibility and extract structured information with source-level provenance. A stratified subset will be manually coded and independently checked to form a gold-standard benchmark. AI outputs will be evaluated using retrieval recall, precision, F1 score, field-level accuracy, numerical error, unsupported-claim rate, processing cost and time. One substantive XR hypothesis will then be preregistered and tested using only fields that meet predetermined reliability thresholds. Sensitivity analyses will compare conclusions based on AI-extracted and human-verified data. The system will retain publication identifiers, supporting source passages, confidence values and processing logs.
