## Project summary

Gamma oscillations are a long-studied feature of brain activity and have been posited as a general-purpose mechanism for inter-regional communication. My work focuses on how features of these neuronal oscillations may reveal aspects of brain state, and therefore be useful as a biomarker for neurological disorders or mental health conditions. However, the brain does not exist in isolation; neurophysiology is inextricably coupled to other states of the body, for example arousal. Such states can be inferred by recording activity outside the brain, for example, the heartbeat as recorded with ECG, or the pupil diameter as recorded with an eyetracker. In this project, you will analyse existing MEG data recorded at CHBH to investigate the relationship between gamma oscillations, heart beat and pupil diameter. You will test whether such peripheral physiological indices exert an effect on the amplitude, frequency and/or waveform shape of gamma oscillations (the specifics of this can be determined between student and supervisor). This work will lay the foundations for a larger research grant proposal about gamma oscillations as a neuro-marker.
