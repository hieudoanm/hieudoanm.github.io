# Testing the Double Empathy Framework: Autistic and non-autistic communication of emotional experiences.

## Project ID

## Project title

Testing the Double Empathy Framework: Autistic and non-autistic communication of emotional experiences.

## Project summary

This study aims to investigate cross-neurotype emotional communication and empirically test the Double Empathy Framework. Using an online task, autistic and non-autistic adults will watch autistic and non-autistic narrators deliver emotional narratives and rate their emotions and first impressions of narrators. We would like to better understand predictors of communication and first impression success from these video stimuli and use this to inform an in-person, live interaction study. The live interaction study will be an opportunity for students to gain hands on experience in measuring subjective, behavioural and physiological synchrony between pairs of individuals of the same or different neurotypes.

The Double Empathy Framework suggests that autistic people experience social challenges due to a bi-directional breakdown in communication between two different minds (i.e. autistic and non-autistic). Empirical research into autistic people’s communication inspired by this framework has been mixed, with some studies finding an advantage for same-neurotype communication, while others suggest communication success does not neatly depend on dyad neurotype. This project will help us to better understand factors that play into communication success and first impression judgments for autistic and non-autistic adults, whilst contributing to new methodological approaches to evaluating live pair interactions.
