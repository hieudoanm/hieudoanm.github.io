## Project title

Assessing reading motivation in young people using experimental measures

## Project summary

In our highly literate society, being good at reading is essential for thriving in education and employment. Yet, children and young people’s engagement with reading is in crisis: National Literacy Trust (NLT) annual UK literacy survey data shows the lowest levels of reading enjoyment since 2005 (when their annual surveys began). Young people’s motivation to read is often measured using questionnaires, with the clear limitation that they often find it difficult to accurately estimate their reading behaviours (e.g. Cremin & Scholes, 2024). Recent experimental work with adults has shown that task-based measures can provide more insight into the decision-making process of choosing to read for pleasure, allowing us to assess dynamic changes in enjoyment during reading (Bains et al., 2023).

The goal of this project is to adapt and test the efficacy of a willingness-to-wait paradigm among children to gain more insight into underlying mechanisms of choosing to read for pleasure in children and young people, providing more objective measurements of reading enjoyment, and providing insight into fluctuations in participant motivation to read.
