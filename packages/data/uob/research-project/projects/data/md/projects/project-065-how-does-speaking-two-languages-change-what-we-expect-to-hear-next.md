## Project title

How does speaking two languages change what we expect to hear next?

## Project summary

This project will investigate how bilingual experience shapes the predictability of words in sentence context. Participants across the adult lifespan with a wide range of language backgrounds, including different first and second languages, will complete sentences with the first word that comes to mind. These responses will be used to estimate cloze probability; how strongly a sentence context leads people to expect a particular word. Rather than simply comparing “monolingual” and “bilingual” groups, the project will examine bilingual experience as a continuum, considering factors such as proficiency, age of acquisition, frequency of language use and language switching.  The study will also include measures of cognitive control to test whether differences in the ability to manage competing information are related to how strongly people use sentence context to generate predictions of upcoming words, and whether these relationships change with age. The student will contribute to an ongoing programme of research examining how language experience, cognitive control and ageing shape prediction across the adult lifespan.

## Topic

Bilingualism, Ageing, Language Comprehension, Prediction

## Methodology

Online and/or in-person sentence-completion study using Qualtrics; language-experience questionnaires; bilingual experience measures, cognitive-control tasks; behavioural data collection; quantitative analysis in R using mixed-effects models.
