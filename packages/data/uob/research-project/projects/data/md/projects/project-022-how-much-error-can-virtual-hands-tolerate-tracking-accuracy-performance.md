## Project title

How Much Error Can Virtual Hands Tolerate? Tracking Accuracy, Performance and Body Ownership in VR

## Project summary

Hand tracking enables natural interaction in virtual reality, but tracking errors may reduce performance, perceived control and ownership of the virtual hand. This project asks how much systematic spatial error users can tolerate before interaction deteriorates. Participants will complete object-selection and grasping tasks in VR while small, controlled offsets or jitter are introduced into hand position. Accuracy, completion time and movement corrections will be combined with ratings of agency, ownership and usability. The study contributes to ongoing VR Lab research on hand tracking, sensorimotor interaction and measurement methods. A focused core experiment will be suitable for Psychology Conversion students, while an optional kinematic or modelling extension can be undertaken by students with computational interests. Existing equipment, starter software and analysis templates will be provided. The project may contribute to a preregistered open dataset and a subsequent publication.

## Topic

Virtual reality; hand tracking; body ownership; agency; sensorimotor interaction; psychophysics; human-computer interaction

## Methodology

Quantitative within-participant VR experiment. One hand-tracking perturbation will be manipulated systematically across conditions. Primary outcomes will be task accuracy and/or completion time; secondary outcomes will include movement corrections and validated ratings of agency, ownership and usability. Sample size will be determined by an a priori power analysis. The analysis will use repeated-measures or mixed-effects models, with optional kinematic modelling for a computational extension. The study will be preregistered and practical data collection will be designed for completion by May.
