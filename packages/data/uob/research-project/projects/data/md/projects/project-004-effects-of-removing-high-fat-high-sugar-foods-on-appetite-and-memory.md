## Project title

Effects of removing high fat high sugar foods on appetite and memory

## Project summary

The proposed study will investigate whether cutting out foods high in fat and sugar (HFS) can improve brain function and appetite control in young adults who regularly consume these foods. Previous research suggests that even a short exposure to HFS foods can negatively affect memory, particularly memory processes linked to the hippocampus, a brain region important for learning and appetite regulation. Poor memory can make it harder to remember what has been eaten, leading to overeating and reduced feelings of fullness. This may create a cycle where unhealthy eating harms memory, which in turn encourages further unhealthy eating and weight gain. While many dietary studies have focused on adding healthy foods to the diet, few have examined whether simply reducing HFS foods can improve brain health. This study addresses that gap by testing whether removing these foods from the diets of young, healthy, habitual consumers leads to measurable improvements in memory, appetite control, and other aspects of cognitive functioning. The findings could help identify a practical way to support both brain health and healthier eating habits early in life.
