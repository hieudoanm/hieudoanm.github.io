## Project title

Measuring Experiences of the Double Empathy Problem

## Project summary

Empathy is the ability to understand and feel the emotions of other people. Autistic people are often characterised as having less empathy for others, and regularly score lower on traditional measures of empathy. Milton (2012) highlighted how a breakdown in mutual understanding (an empathy "error") occurs across a dyad (not within an individual), and that this is more likely in pairs of people with a greater disparity in their native communication and interaction dispositions. As such, empathy problems are not problems "for" autistic people, but rather for autistic and non-autistic dyads. One corrollary of this is that (as a neurominority), autistic people should not only have regular experiences of struggling to understand and feel the emotions of others, but also of feeling misunderstood or "unfelt" by others. Qualtative work suggests this is the case, however, there are few quantitative ways to measure this. This project will convert existing questionnaire measures of empathy to measuring the experience of empathy felt from others, examine their psychometric properties and correlates in autistic and non-autistic participants.
