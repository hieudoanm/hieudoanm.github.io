## Project title

Examining brain state variability with fNIRS and fMRI

## Project summary

The majority of the brain’s physiological and metabolic activity is concerned with intrinsic processes, not responses to external stimulation. Even robust sensory stimuli lead to relatively minor perturbations to the system, as the brain constantly adapts its processing to meet instantaneous and competing internal, environmental and behavioural demands. This observation rarely features in neuroimaging analyses, which erroneously assume a dissociation between stimulus responses and ongoing brain activity. The consequence of this is that the majority of the signal is considered to be noise and rejected, which could be a significant contributing factor to why functional neuroimaging of individual participants is not considered robust. Functional variability is not noise: a considerable proportion of it can be explained by the dynamics of ongoing brain activity, or ‘brain state’, and it is predictive of behaviour, pathology and development through the lifespan. This project will work with existing projects acquiring functional near infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) and functional MRI (fMRI) data and examine signal variability, with a particular application to the relationship with ADHD and sleep traits.
