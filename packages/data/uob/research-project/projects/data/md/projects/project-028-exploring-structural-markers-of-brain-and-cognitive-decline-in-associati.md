## Project title

Exploring structural markers of brain and cognitive decline in association with modifiable lifestyle factors (e.g. sleep, education)

## Project summary

Numerous studies indicate large inter-individual differences in cognitive ageing, specifically in susceptibility to age-related cognitive decline (normal healthy ageing) and to disease-related pathology (dementia) or mental health problems. With an increasingly ageing world population, the key research challenges include understanding factors supporting maintenance of cognitive functioning in old age (good sleep, physical activity, education etc) and uncovering brain markers associated with high risk of cognitive decline and mental health problems in elderly population. The age-related cognitive decline is known to be associated with changes within neuronal networks, including altered structural connectivity, brain atrophy within various cortical and subcortical areas, cerebrovascular changes and functional networks dynamics. Increasing evidence suggests that lifetime cognitive stimulation compensates for age-related brain changes and accounts for differences in age-related cognitive decline. On the other hand, changes in sleep patterns/sleep loss (common complaint in older adults) contribute to age-related cognitive decline and affect mental health. The MSc project will offer opportunity to use advanced magnetic resonance imaging techniques (e.g., diffusion MRI based measures of connectivity, brain age estimation, cortical and subcortical parcellations, volumetric analysis) to explore brain ageing and to gain insights into potential factors underlying heterogeneity in cognitive ageing. The project will be based on secondary MRI data analysis BUT will offer opportunity to get involved in MRI data collection alongside ongoing projects. You will have a chance to propose and design your own project based on available datasets (e.g., CamCAN, ADNI).

For students with extensive prior computational skills, there will be also a potential to work on projects in collaboration with my colleagues from Mathematics (Enrico Amico) or Computer Science (Melanie Jouaiti). This will need to be discussed and agreed on one-to-one basis. If you are interested please email or arrange a meeting to discuss.
