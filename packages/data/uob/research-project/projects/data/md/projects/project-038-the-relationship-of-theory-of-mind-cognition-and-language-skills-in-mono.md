# The relationship of Theory of Mind, cognition and language skills in monolingual and bilingual children

## Project title

The relationship of Theory of Mind, cognition and language skills in monolingual and bilingual children

## Project summary

Theory of Mind (ToM) is the ability to attribute mental states (e.g., beliefs desires, and emotions) to others to predict or explain their behaviour. Various studies have examined the association between ToM development and development of other cognitive functions, for instance meta-linguistic awareness (MLA). The latter the conscious reflection on the structural features of language (e.g., sounds, morphological units, and syntactic structures), treating language forms as objects of thought rather than focusing on their communicative meaning. Both MLA and ToM undergo substantial growth during early and middle childhood, and both require children to step back from a dominant perspective and think about abstract representations, whether linguistic or mental. But the relationship between ToM and MLA as well as its relationship with wider cognitive skills remains unclear. In addition, the relationship appears to be affected by children's experience with more than one language. The current project therefore investigates relationships between ToM, MLA and cognition within monolingual and bilingual children (~3-5 year olds).
