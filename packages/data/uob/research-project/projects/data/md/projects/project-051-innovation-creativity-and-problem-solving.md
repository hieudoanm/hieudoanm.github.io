## Project title

Innovation, creativity, and problem solving

## Project summary

Imagine trying to rescue your keys that have fallen behind a radiator, perhaps you might use a ruler or a coat-hanger to try to reach them. This is one example where we encounter a problem that could be solved with creative use of a tool. Humans are often thought to be expert tool users with few non-human animals having the capacity to use tools innovatively. However, this is challenged by evidence that young children are remarkably poor at solving problems using tools, whereas some non-human animals (crows, cockatoos, great apes) show impressive performance  (e.g. Beck et al., 2011; Beck, 2016). Innovating a tool is a complex challenge that is likely to involve executive functions, creativity (such as divergent thinking), and imagination.
In this project we will look at adults' ability to flexibly innovate solutions to problems and the relationship between success on problem solving tasks and other cognitive and creativity measures. If students are interested it may be possible to explore the relation between these constructs and neurodiversity traits.
The project is a small group project where you will collaborate on data collection. Individual students will develop their own research questions and measures.
