## Project title

What Does AI Think of Me? How AI-Generated Feedback Shapes Self-Beliefs

## Project summary

From asking ChatGPT whether an email sounds rude, to seeking advice about restaurant recommendations and career choices, people are increasingly turning to artificial intelligence (AI) not just for information, but also for feedback about themselves. As AI becomes more personalised and conversational, an important question is whether its evaluations can actually shape how we see ourselves (i.e., Self belief).

Research in cognitive psychology has shown that people continuously form and revise beliefs about their own abilities based on feedback from others. However, this process is often biased: we may give more weight to feedback that confirms what we already believe, while discounting information that challenges our existing self-view. But what happens when that feedback comes from AI rather than another person? How about when the AI is more generic vs more personalised? Do we trust it less because “it is only an algorithm”, or more because AI can appear objective, knowledgeable, and unbiased?

In this project, the student will use an experimental task in which participants receive positive and negative evaluations of their abilities from either an AI system or a human evaluator. We will examine how these evaluations influence self-beliefs over time, and whether AI changes the way people respond to confirming versus conflicting feedback. Alongside behavioural analysis, the project can use computational modelling to estimate how strongly people learn from different types of feedback and whether these learning processes differ for AI and human sources. This project offers a timely way to study how AI may increasingly shape everyday self-perception and decision-making.
