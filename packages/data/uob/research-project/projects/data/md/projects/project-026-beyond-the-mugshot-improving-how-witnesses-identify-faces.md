## Project title

Beyond the Mugshot: Improving How Witnesses Identify Faces

## Project summary

This project explores how artificial intelligence can be used to improve decisions about human faces in high-stakes settings such as eyewitness identification, identity verification and border security. You will investigate how factors such as viewing angle, lighting and disguise influence face recognition, and whether behavioural signals including eye movements, hesitation and patterns of visual exploration can help predict when an identification is likely to be accurate. Using experimental psychology, eye-tracking and computational methods, the project will examine how adaptive AI systems could personalise the way faces are presented and support more reliable human decision-making. The research sits at the cutting edge of psychology and AI, with clear potential to inform forensic practice, policing and the development of responsible technologies for person identification. You will join an international team of researchers collecting complementary data across sites, including collaborators at the Max Planck Institute, the University of Victoria and the University of Stirling, giving you the opportunity to work within a genuinely interdisciplinary and international research programme.

## Topic

episodic memory, eyewitness memory, criminal identification

## Methodology

The project will use established eyewitness identification methods to examine how people recognise and identify faces under different conditions. Students will conduct controlled experiments using target-present and target-absent lineups, manipulating factors such as viewing angle, lighting and disguise, and measuring identification accuracy, confidence and decision time. Analyses will draw on signal detection and confidence–accuracy approaches to assess discriminability and the reliability of identification decisions.
