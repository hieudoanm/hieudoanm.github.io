## Project title

Can a Virtual Musician Keep You in Time? Synchronisation and Social Presence in Virtual Ensembles

## Project summary

Remote and virtual music systems must support both accurate synchronisation and a convincing sense of performing with another person. This project will investigate how the timing behaviour of a virtual musician affects a participant's ability to maintain a shared rhythm and their experience of social presence. Participants will perform a simple tapping task with a virtual musical partner. The partner will respond adaptively to the participant, follow a fixed tempo, or exhibit controlled timing variability. The study will measure synchronisation error, variability, adaptation following timing changes, perceived responsiveness and social presence. Musical training will not be required. The project will build on existing VR Lab work, software and datasets concerning ensemble synchronisation, ARME and Virtuoso Strings. A supported behavioural-analysis route will be suitable for Psychology Conversion students, with optional time-series or computational modelling for students with relevant experience. The project may contribute to a preregistered dataset and a subsequent publication.

## Topic

Music cognition; interpersonal synchronisation; virtual musicians; social presence; timing; computational modelling; virtual reality

## Methodology

Quantitative within-participant behavioural experiment using a simple rhythmic tapping task with a virtual partner. Partner behaviour will vary across adaptive, fixed-tempo and controlled-variability conditions. Primary outcomes will be mean absolute asynchrony and/or synchronisation variability; secondary outcomes will include phase correction, perceived responsiveness and social presence. Sample size will be based on an a priori power analysis. Repeated-measures or mixed-effects analyses will test condition effects, with an optional time-series or Kalman-filter modelling extension. The task and recruitment plan will be designed so that data collection can be completed by May.
