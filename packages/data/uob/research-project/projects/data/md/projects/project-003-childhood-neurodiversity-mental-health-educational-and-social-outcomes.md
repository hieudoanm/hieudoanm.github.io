## Project title

Childhood neurodiversity, mental health, educational and social outcomes

## Project summary

Emerging evidence indicates that children’s mental health is linked with both educational and social outcomes (e.g., Morris et al., 2021). Understanding the role of children’s mental health and neurodiversity in later outcomes is important because cross-national studies indicate that approximately 1 in 15 (7.6%) 5- to 9-year-old children have a mental health or neurodevelopmental condition (WHO, 2022). Remarkably, few studies have investigated either the overlap between mental health and neurodiversity in primary school-aged children or the developmental impact of mental health and neurodiversity on children’s later educational and social outcomes. The over-arching aim of this project is to examine the developmental impact of childhood mental health and neurodiversity on later social and educational outcomes.

The project uses existing data from a large-scale longitudinal study of approximately 8000 children tracked across four time points between ages 9 to 20 years. The dataset includes measures of children’s mental health, neurodiversity, cognitive ability, academic performance, educational attainment, and friendship and peer relationships. The dataset also contains rich information on the socio-demographic characteristics of participants and their families.

This project will suit students who are interested in developmental science, mental health, and neurodiversity. The project is relevant to those who hope to develop their quantitative data analysis skills in structural equation modelling and longitudinal data analysis. Advanced quantitative data analysis skills are highly desired by employers and are particularly important for those hoping to pursue further academic research (e.g., Ph.D.). This project is not suitable for students taking the MSc Psychology (Conversion) or MSc Philosophy, Mental Health and Psychology.
