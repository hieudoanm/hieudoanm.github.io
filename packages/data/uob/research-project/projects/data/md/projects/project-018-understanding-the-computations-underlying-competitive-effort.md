## Project title

Understanding the computations underlying competitive effort.

## Project summary

From beating your friends while gaming, to entering a writing competition, everyday we engage in social interactions where we compete with others. Research examining competition has often used economic games to understand the cognitive processes underlying how motivated people are when competing with others. In such experiments people are often competing on single trials to beat a single other opponent. Yet, while useful, such an approach misses many things that happens when we compete in the real world. For instance, when racing against others in a ParkRun, or several of our friends on Mario Kart or Gran Turismo, many other computations might impact on how motivated we are to engage in the competition. For instance, the competitions occurs over extended periods of time, rather than being a single-shot effort, during which motivation might fluctuate considerably. Moreover, If we are behind an opponent we might work harder to get in front, compared to if they are miles ahead of us, and we might be more motivated when competing with a smaller number of people than a large group. However, little research has been conducted that offers a an account of the computations that people engage in when motivating themselves during a competition that occurs over an extended period of time against multiple opponents. In this project the student will work with Prof. Apps and Dr. Hikaru Sugimoto, using an experimental task in which people have to make decisions about how much physical effort to exert when competing against other opponents in a race. Using computational modelling we will dissect the cognitive mechanisms that underlie competition and how they impact on the motivation to exert effort. This project will offer a unique perspective on the psychology of competition.
