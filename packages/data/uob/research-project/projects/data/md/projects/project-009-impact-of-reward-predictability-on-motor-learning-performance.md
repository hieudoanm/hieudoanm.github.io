## Project title

Impact of reward predictability on motor learning/performance

## Project summary

It is well established that reward can enhance motor performance: it can “break” the usual speed–accuracy trade-off, leading to movements that are both faster and more accurate. This has important implications for designing interventions to optimise motor function in both elite performers and individuals undergoing rehabilitation. Classic work by Skinner (1957) suggests that unpredictable (variable) reward schedules are particularly effective for improving behaviour and supporting memory consolidation. However, almost all recent human studies in both healthy and clinical populations have relied on predictable (continuous) reward schedules. This project asks: Does an unpredictable reward schedule amplify the beneficial effects of reward on motor performance? To address this, we will compare groups exposed to different reward schedules while they perform a reaching task in which both movement speed and accuracy are precisely measured.
