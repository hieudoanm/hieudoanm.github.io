## Project title

Memory Under Pressure: Can Digital Evidence Distort Eyewitness Testimony?

## Project summary

Eyewitnesses today can encounter a flood of information after an event, from social media and news reports to CCTV images and information shared by investigators. But can people still distinguish what they actually witnessed from what they learned afterwards? This project will investigate how post-event information affects memory and source monitoring, and whether interviewing techniques can help witnesses preserve the accuracy of their accounts. Students will contribute to a wider programme of research developing new approaches to protecting eyewitness memory in high-stakes investigations.

## Topic

memory, investigative interviewing, eyewitness memory

## Methodology

The project will use controlled eyewitness-memory experiments in which participants experience an event and subsequently encounter accurate, neutral or misleading post-event information. Students may measure free recall, memory accuracy and source attribution, testing whether people can distinguish what they witnessed from information encountered later. Depending on the specific question, experiments may use immersive VR scenarios and different interviewing or source-monitoring techniques.
