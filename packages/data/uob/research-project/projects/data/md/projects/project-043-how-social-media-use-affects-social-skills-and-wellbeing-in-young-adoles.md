## Project title

How Social Media Use Affects Social Skills and Wellbeing in Young Adolescents

## Project summary

Social media plays an increasingly important role in young people’s social, emotional, and psychological development. While social media can provide opportunities for communication, self-expression, and social connection, concerns remain regarding its potential impact on mental health, peer relationships, and the development of social skills during early adolescence. Understanding how different patterns of social media use relate to young people’s wellbeing and social functioning is particularly important, given that early adolescence represents a critical developmental period characterised by substantial social and emotional change.
The overarching aim of this project is to examine the associations between social media use, social skills, and mental health in young adolescents. Using a cross-sectional survey design, the study will collect data from 11-year-olds. More than 400 participants will complete self-report measures assessing social media use, online experiences, mental health and wellbeing, social competence, friendship quality, and peer relationships. The study will also gather information on relevant demographic and family characteristics to better understand factors associated with positive and negative outcomes.
This project will suit students interested in developmental psychology, social development, and the role of digital technologies in young people’s lives. Students will gain experience in survey design, psychological measurement, data management, and quantitative data analysis. Depending on the student’s interests and experience, analyses may include multiple regression, mediation, moderation, and structural equation modelling techniques. These analytical skills are highly valued by employers and provide an excellent foundation for students considering further postgraduate research, including PhD study.
This project is particularly suitable for students interested in understanding how digital environments shape young people’s development and wellbeing, and who wish to contribute to evidence that can inform educational, clinical, and policy initiatives aimed at promoting healthy digital engagement among adolescents.
