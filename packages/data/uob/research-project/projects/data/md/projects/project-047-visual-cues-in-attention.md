Visual cues in attention

## Project summary

Humans are remarkably sensitive to the spatial cues around them, from a simple arrow or the word “LEFT” to the subtle shift of someone’s gaze or the direction of a pointing hand. These visual cues act like silent guides, steering our attention and helping us react more quickly and accurately to what’s happening in our surroundings. But not everyone responds to these cues in the same way. Factors like gender, dominance, race, emotional state, neurodiversity, and cognitive load shape how we interpret and act on these signals.

Moreover, not all cues are created equal: while some, like gaze direction, tap into largely automatic processes, others such as symbolic arrows rely on learned associations and may engage different cognitive processes. Additionally, it turns out that these visual cues don’t operate in isolation. They often interact with sounds and other sensory input, creating complex, cross-modal effects that further influence perception.

This research project aims to uncover how and why people process visual cues differently, and what this reveals about the hidden biases shaping our perception.
