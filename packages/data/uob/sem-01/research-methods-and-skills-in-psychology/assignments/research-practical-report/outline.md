- **Evidence to consult:** Seed review and broad method reviews for framing and terminology. ([Forstmann et al. ### 2. What the method models and how it works (~250–300 words)

- Explain the core idea in your own words: noisy information is accumulated over time until a decision criterion is reached; the selected boundary corresponds to the response, and the time to reach it contributes to the response time. ([Forstmann et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645); [Ratcliff et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.01.007))
- Walk through a single illustrative trial in sequence: stimulus or choice options appear; evidence is sampled; evidence is accumulated; a boundary is reached; the response is initiated. Distinguish the model's latent process from directly observed choices and response times.
- Introduce only the parameters used or taught in the practical. For a standard two-boundary DDM, check whether the report needs to explain:
  - **Drift rate:** average direction/rate of evidence accumulation; explain how its sign or magnitude maps to stimulus quality, option value, or the practical's manipulation. ([Forstmann et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645); [Ratcliff et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.01.007))
  - **Boundary separation / threshold:** amount of evidence required before responding and its relationship to caution and the speed–accuracy trade-off. Avoid treating it as a direct measure of a single personality trait. ([Forstmann et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645); [Ratcliff et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.01.007); [van Ravenzwaaij et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1081-y))
  - **Starting point / bias:** initial position between the response boundaries and how it can represent a response preference before evidence accumulation. ([Forstmann et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645); [Ratcliff et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.01.007); [Zhao et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2020.101331))
  - **Non-decision time:** time assigned to encoding and response execution outside the modeled accumulation period; describe its interpretation cautiously because the model does not separately observe those stages. ([Forstmann et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645); [Ratcliff et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.01.007))
  - **Across-trial variability or within-trial noise:** include only if the practical's model estimated these terms; define the relevant variability and note that added complexity affects estimation. ([Boehm et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2018.09.004); [Fard et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2017.00029); [White et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-017-1271-2))
- Explain how the model jointly uses correct and error responses and the distributions of response times, rather than only their averages. Use the seed review for the general account and avoid adding mathematical notation unless the practical requires it. ([Forstmann et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645); [Myers et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1039172); [Ratcliff et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.01.007))
- **Optional figure:** Draw evidence paths to two boundaries, mark starting point, drift direction, boundary separation, and non-decision time. Make the figure yourself and align it with the exact model variant used.
- **Possible evidence/examples:** Reviews and tutorial for core description; a relevant application only if it matches the practical. ([Axt & Johnson (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2021.104134); [Bolam et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65549-5); [Chau et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.12273); [Forstmann et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645); [Myers et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1039172); [Pérez-Parra & Rojas-Líbano (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1080/02109395.2022.2056802); [Ratcliff et al. - Use a table or figure only if it improves clarity and is permitted. Refer to it in text and do not duplicate all its values in prose.

### 12. Discussion (~120–160 words)

- Answer the practical's research question using the observed results, then explain what the result suggests about the relevant DDM component or process. Keep the inference proportional to the data and model.
- Compare with one or two relevant sources, explaining whether the tasks and methods are sufficiently similar for the comparison. ### 13. Conclusion (~40–60 words)

- Give a brief final answer about what the method contributed to understanding the practical's question and the main qualification on that conclusion.
- Do not introduce new evidence, repeat the complete results, or claim that the practical proves a mechanism.

## References and presentation checklist

- Use the reference style specified by the practical lead; otherwise choose a recognised style and apply it consistently. The bibliography uses APA-style entries; do not assume APA is required unless the lead confirms it.
- Cite sources in text in the required style and include only cited items in the final reference list. Check every author/year/DOI against [bibliography.md](bibliography.md) and the linked source.
- Use primary empirical papers for specific findings and reviews/tutorials for broad explanations. Distinguish the paper's result from your interpretation of it.
- Add the cover sheet, report title, practical name, page numbers, and any required declarations.
- Apply consistent typeface, double spacing, and heading hierarchy, as directed by the brief.
- Ask the practical lead whether an appendix is needed and what material belongs there.
- Proofread for accuracy, concise academic language, inclusive wording, typos, citation/reference matching, and formatting consistency.
