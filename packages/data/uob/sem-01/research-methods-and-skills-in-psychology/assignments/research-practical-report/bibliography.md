# Drift diffusion model: candidate bibliography

This is a candidate source pool for planning and further reading, not a requirement to cite every item in the report. It contains **50 references** (expanded source pool); all were published in **2016 or later**, and sources published in **2021 or later are preferred**. The list prioritises peer-reviewed DDM research, methodological studies, and reviews. Retain only sources that directly support the final report and verify practical-specific claims against the provided materials.

## Abstract summaries

_Concise paraphrases of the cited works’ abstracts; these are summaries, not verbatim abstracts._

### 1. [Comparing eight parameter estimation methods for the Ratcliff diffusion model using free software](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.484737)

Compares eight freely available parameter-estimation procedures for the Ratcliff diffusion model using simulation, focusing on how well each method recovers known parameters under different data conditions.

### 2. [Understanding mechanisms behind discrimination using diffusion decision modeling](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2021.104134)

Applies the DDM to discrimination judgments to separate candidate decision mechanisms and examine how interventions affect the processes that produce discriminatory choices.

### 3. [Estimating across-trial variability parameters of the diffusion decision model: Expert advice and recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2018.09.004)

Reviews challenges in estimating across-trial variability in the DDM and offers expert recommendations for model specification, estimation, and reporting.

### 4. [A drift diffusion model analysis of age-related impact on multisensory decision-making processes](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65549-5)

In a sample spanning ages 18–90, older adults showed slower evidence accumulation; audiovisual information improved response time relative to visual-only trials, with evidence that multisensory accumulation benefits varied by task difficulty and age.

### 5. [Expressions for Bayesian confidence of drift diffusion observers in fluctuating stimuli tasks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2023.102815)

Derives computationally efficient Bayesian expressions for confidence reports in DDM tasks with fluctuating evidence and time-varying decision thresholds.

### 6. [Hierarchical drift diffusion modeling uncovers multisensory benefit in numerosity discrimination tasks](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.12273)

Uses hierarchical DDMs to test whether combining auditory and visual information benefits numerosity decisions, showing how the approach can work with a modest dataset.

### 7. [An EZ Bayesian hierarchical drift diffusion model for response time and accuracy](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-025-02729-y)

Presents an EZ Bayesian hierarchical DDM for jointly modelling response time and accuracy, with simulations assessing parameter estimation and recovery.

### 8. [Multiple timescales of learning indicated by changes in evidence-accumulation processes during perceptual decision-making](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00168-9)

Tracks evidence-accumulation parameters over several days of perceptual learning to distinguish the time courses of changes in evidence quality and response caution.

### 9. [The gated cascade diffusion model: An integrated theory of decision making, motor preparation, and motor execution](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000464)

Introduces a gated cascade diffusion model that links evidence-based choice with motor preparation and execution in an integrated account of response time.

### 10. [Using drift diffusion and reinforcement learning models to disentangle effects of depression on decision-making versus learning in the probabilistic reward task](https://doi.org/10.5334/cpsy.108)

Compares drift-diffusion and reinforcement-learning analyses of probabilistic reward-task data to distinguish decision-making differences from learning effects in depression.

### 11. [The quality of response time data inference: A blinded, collaborative assessment of the validity of cognitive models](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-017-1417-2)

Seventeen teams independently analysed 14 response-time datasets; the authors compared the resulting inferences to assess how analytic choices affect conclusions drawn from cognitive models.

### 12. [A Bayesian reformulation of the extended drift-diffusion model in perceptual decision making](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2017.00029)

Reformulates the extended DDM in Bayesian terms and examines how trial-to-trial variability in the model can be represented in that framework.

### 13. [An attentional drift diffusion model over binary-attribute choice](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2017.06.007)

Proposes an attentional DDM for choices between alternatives described by binary attributes and tests how attention relates to choice and response time.

### 14. [A multiattribute attentional drift diffusion model](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2021.04.004)

Develops a multiattribute attentional DDM and tests how visual attention to options and attributes relates to choices and response times.

### 15. [A reinforcement learning diffusion decision model for value-based decisions](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-018-1554-2)

Combines reinforcement learning with a diffusion choice rule so the model can represent both value learning and the time course of value-based decisions.

### 16. [Sequential sampling models in cognitive neuroscience: Advantages, applications, and extensions](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033645)

Reviews sequential-sampling models in cognitive neuroscience, describing their advantages, applications, neural interpretations, and extensions.

### 17. [Testing the drift-diffusion model](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2011446117)

Develops identification results and a statistical test for determining whether observed choice and response-time data are consistent with a drift-diffusion model.

### 18. [Basal ganglia components have distinct computational roles in decision-making dynamics under conflict and uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002978)

Combines neural recordings and diffusion modelling to investigate how basal-ganglia components contribute to decision dynamics under conflict and uncertainty.

### 19. [Neural substrates of the drift-diffusion model in brain disorders](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2021.678232)

Reviews proposed neural substrates of DDM parameters and research applying the model to brain disorders.

### 20. [Temporal dynamics of decision making: A synthesis of computational and neurophysiological approaches](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1586)

Synthesizes computational and neurophysiological approaches to the temporal unfolding of decision-making.

### 21. [Improving parameter recovery for conflict drift-diffusion models](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-020-01366-8)

Shows that grid-search selection of starting values can improve fitting and parameter recovery for conflict DDMs, particularly for more complex model variants.

### 22. [The counterintuitive impact of responses and response times on parameter estimates in the drift diffusion model](https://doi.org/10.1111/bmsp.12183)

Analyses how individual responses and response times can influence maximum-likelihood DDM parameter estimates, highlighting the impact of unusually fast observations.

### 23. [Regulation of craving training to support healthy food choices under stress: A randomized control trial employing the hierarchical drift-diffusion model](https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12522)

In a randomized study of 118 employees, craving-regulation training increased healthy food choices both with and without stress; HDDM analyses found intervention-related differences in non-decision time and starting bias, particularly in the no-stress condition.

### 24. [Value certainty in drift-diffusion models of preferential choice](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000329)

Develops DDM extensions that represent uncertainty about option values and compares how value certainty can shape choice consistency and response time.

### 25. [Thinking more or thinking differently? Using drift-diffusion modeling to illuminate why accuracy prompts decrease misinformation sharing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105312)

Uses DDM analyses to show that accuracy prompts reduce misinformation sharing by changing information processing (including evidence evaluation), rather than simply increasing the amount of deliberation.

### 26. [Emotion-driven moral evaluation: A mechanistic study based on the drift-diffusion model](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci14101005)

Uses a drift-diffusion framework to study the decision processes involved in emotion-related moral evaluation.

### 27. [Does attention increase the value of choice alternatives?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2021.01.004)

Reviews evidence on whether visual attention changes the subjective value of choice alternatives and evaluates assumptions of attentional DDM accounts.

### 28. [A practical introduction to using the drift diffusion model of decision-making in cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and health sciences](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1039172)

Introduces DDM fundamentals and gives a worked example covering fitting, model validation, interpretation, and reporting across psychology, neuroscience, and health research.

### 29. [Optimizing sequential decisions in the drift-diffusion model](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2018.11.001)

Studies optimal sequential decisions in the DDM, connecting decision policies and thresholds to performance over time.

### 30. [How attention influences perceptual decision making: Single-trial EEG correlates of drift-diffusion model parameters](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2016.03.003)

Relates single-trial EEG measures of attention to drift-diffusion parameters in a perceptual decision task.

### 31. [Cognitive models of decision-making with identifiable parameters: Diffusion decision models with within-trial noise](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2025.102917)

Examines when DDM parameters, including within-trial noise, can be identified from behavioural data and how additional neural or covariate measures may help.

### 32. [A drift-diffusion model of temporal generalization outperforms existing models and captures modality differences and learning effects](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02819-8)

Introduces a DDM for temporal generalization judgments and compares its fit and parameter recovery with existing models across modality and learning studies.

### 33. [dockerHDDM: A user-friendly environment for Bayesian hierarchical drift-diffusion modeling](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459241298700)

Describes dockerHDDM, a packaged environment intended to make Bayesian hierarchical drift-diffusion modelling easier to install and use.

### 34. [What can evidence accumulation modelling tell us about human social cognition?](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218231176950)

Reviews how evidence-accumulation models can inform social-cognition research and discusses assumptions and practical modelling considerations.

### 35. [The drift diffusion model as the choice rule in reinforcement learning](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1199-y)

Proposes a reinforcement-learning model that uses the DDM as its choice mechanism, compares candidate learning and decision mechanisms against choice and response-time data, and applies the model to stimulant effects in ADHD.

### 36. [Drift-diffusion cognitive models: Description, applications and perspectives](https://doi.org/10.1080/02109395.2022.2056802)

Provides an overview of drift-diffusion cognitive models, their applications, and perspectives for research use.

### 37. [Diffusion decision model: Current issues and history](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.01.007)

Reviews the history and current issues of the DDM, including its core components, extensions, tests, and uses across decision tasks.

### 38. [Decision making on spatially continuous scales](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000117)

Presents and evaluates a diffusion model for decisions on continuous spatial scales, extending evidence accumulation beyond a simple two-choice response.

### 39. [Integrated diffusion models for distance effects in number memory](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2022.101516)

Integrates models of number representation with diffusion decision processes to explain distance effects in number memory and related choice data.

### 40. [Diffusion models with time-dependent parameters: An analysis of computational effort and accuracy of different numerical methods](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2023.102756)

Compares numerical methods for computing predictions of diffusion models with time-varying parameters, assessing accuracy and computational effort.

### 41. [Drift–diffusion models for multiple-alternative forced-choice decision making](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13408-019-0073-4)

Develops a drift-diffusion account for decisions among more than two alternatives and discusses its theoretical properties and application.

### 42. [Joint modelling of latent cognitive mechanisms shared across decision-making domains](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42113-023-00192-3)

Uses joint hierarchical modelling to test whether latent cognitive mechanisms are shared across multiple decision-making tasks and sessions.

### 43. [Healthful choices depend on the latency and rate of information accumulation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01154-0)

Fits a multi-attribute, time-dependent DDM to dietary choices, finding that taste and health information can enter evidence accumulation with different latencies and rates; the model also captures changes associated with a healthy-choice intervention.

### 44. [The attentional drift diffusion model of simple perceptual decision-making](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2017.00468)

Tests an attentional DDM in simple perceptual tasks to describe how gaze and attention influence choice and response time.

### 45. [Systematic parameter reviews in cognitive modeling: Towards a robust and cumulative characterization of psychological processes in the diffusion decision model](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.608287)

Systematically reviews published DDM parameter estimates and synthesizes them into empirical distributions that can inform priors and future modelling.

### 46. [The EZ diffusion model provides a powerful test of simple empirical effects](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1081-y)

Compares the statistical power of the EZ and full diffusion models for detecting simple experimental effects using simulated data.

### 47. [Childhood adversity is not associated with lowered inhibition, but slower perceptual processing: A drift diffusion model analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogdev.2024.101479)

Finds that childhood adversity was not associated with reduced inhibition in the studied sample, but was associated with slower perceptual processing, as estimated by the DDM.

### 48. [Testing the validity of conflict drift-diffusion models for use in estimating cognitive processes: A parameter-recovery study](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-017-1271-2)

Uses parameter-recovery simulations to test whether conflict drift-diffusion models can recover the cognitive processes they are intended to measure.

### 49. [A dynamic computational model of gaze and choice in multi-attribute decisions](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000350)

Proposes and tests a dynamic model combining gaze and attribute-level attention to explain multiattribute choices and response times.

### 50. [Psychological mechanisms of loss aversion: A drift-diffusion decomposition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2020.101331)

In two preregistered studies of mixed gambles, DDM analyses distinguish utility weighting from rejection biases; a pre-valuation bias explained substantial choice and response-time patterns and could be manipulated separately from valuation.
