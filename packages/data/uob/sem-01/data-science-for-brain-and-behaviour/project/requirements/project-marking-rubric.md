## Question / Task

You should prepare a **1500-word group report** on your project. This should cover the following sections, which you should clearly label in the report. Visualizations are expected in the data and analysis sections but are not a section on their own.

### 1. Defining the Question(s)

Define the specific question you addressed, with brief reference to any background necessary to understand why this question is important.

Defining the question does not require a full literature review as you may have done for practical reports. It should, however, include a more specific introduction to the motivation behind the question you are asking:

- Why are you focusing on this question?
- What makes this an important question to ask?

### 2. Choosing and Characterising Data

Show that you understand the properties of your raw data and explain any merging, cleaning, or adjustment of raw data that has been necessary to produce the dataset that will be the basis for your analysis.

Produce informative visualizations to support understanding of the dataset.

Explain any important properties of your data or choices you have had to make, including their potential impacts.

> **Make sure you explicitly identify the source of your data (e.g. by web address).**

### 3. Analysis

Report your analysis.

> **This must include at least one permutation analysis.**

Do not use black-box techniques. Use the techniques we have learned in class.

You may go on to do further analysis with other techniques, but this is not required. More advanced techniques may add to the project mark if they are clear, accurate, and well explained, but they do not determine a good project mark.

Do not use any techniques you do not fully understand.

A complex analysis using black-box techniques that we do not cover in this course will not earn extra marks. Do not submit analyses that look like they come from a completely different course. **This will fail.**

Do show that you understand your analysis and are fully aware of what you can and cannot conclude from it in relation to the question you have set.

Reflect on both its weaknesses and its strengths.

### 4. Visualizations

Make visualizations that support your analysis and any conclusions you draw.

### 5. Conclusions and Critical Analysis

Draw conclusions. These should be specific and say what you think the evidence shows.

Take a point of view. Do not simply mention different aspects of the data and tell your reader, _"you decide..."_

This does not mean you should ignore weaknesses. They should be highlighted along with strengths.

Critically analyze the conclusions and describe what further work might need to be done, for example:

- What additional or better data would be needed?
- What further analyses could be performed?
